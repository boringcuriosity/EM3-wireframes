Log all, five small controls, examined

All five are small and all five have a 44px tap target, held open by a negative block margin so the row never grows. What separates them is affordance, and where they sit.

The principles disagree with Mobbin on position. Every nutrition app puts its per-meal control in the card header, but their control opens the meal. Ours acts on the food rows. By the Law of Common Region and Uniform Connectedness, a control belongs inside the region of the content it acts on, sharing its edge. That argues for the control sitting with the rows, aligned to them, not up in the header describing the meal.

The affordance trap. On a touch screen there is no hover, so a bare word has only its colour to say it is pressable. Our food names are already tappable and they are near black and bold. Two tappable texts with two different looks breaks the Law of Similarity: same behaviour should look the same. A container solves it; that is what a chip is for.
