The Home screen

What do we say to you
every day, and every hour?

Somebody opens GoodFlip before breakfast, again at four o'clock, again after dinner. Each of those is a chance to say one true thing about that hour. Today the screen answers all three the same way. This is the work to change that.

The insight

We built one half of the loop.

To-do works. It lists the day in the order it happens and a person works down it. What it gives us is adherence. The half still missing is the reason to want to, and that is what the four pillars were always for.

To-do
Adherence

The day as a list. What is on it, when it happens, what is left.

EM3
Motivation

The four pillars as four scores. Where you stand, and which one needs you right now.

Something that helps a person do the task belongs to To-do. Something that makes them want to belongs to EM3.

The concept

Four bubbles

One of them is bigger,
and the hour decides which.

  • 1
    The big one is the pillar that needs you now.
    It carries its score and one line telling you what to do, like "Time to eat".
  • 2
    The other three keep their seats.
    Mind top left, Move top right, Measure bottom right, Eat bottom left. Only the big one changes, so the screen looks the same every time you open it.
  • 3
    Each bubble fills to its own score.
    Four pillars compared at a glance, without reading four numbers.
  • 4
    Kaira sits underneath and joins them up.
    The scores say where you stand and the tasks say what to do. She is the only thing that can say which task moves which score.

They drift a few pixels at rest, each on its own timing, so the cluster feels alive rather than printed. When the middle changes, the old one steps back before the new one comes forward.

The score is the day, not a survey

Eat has seven tasks today and you have logged three, so Eat is at 43. Nothing is set by hand and nothing is guessed. Before you log anything the bubble shows the ask instead of a number, because a percentage of nothing is a figure nobody has earned.

Only what is open right now

A pillar takes the middle only if it has something waiting in the part of the day you are in. That is what keeps the big bubble agreeing with the task list underneath it. Once the day is done all four go back to the same size.

When two are level, the hour decides. Morning belongs to Eat, afternoon to Measure, evening to Move, night to Mind. Breakfast sets the morning, a reading suits the flat middle of the day, the session goes before dinner, and the night is for winding down. It is only ever a tie-break: a pillar genuinely further behind still wins, whatever the hour.

The line underneath

Being specific is what makes it warm.

A line that would work for any person on any day is what makes a system feel like a machine being friendly. Every line Kaira says is built the same way: a read, the one true thing about where this pillar stands, and a lever, the specific act that moves it, with a real number. She talks about the pillar in the big bubble and never about another one.

Reading the screen backYou have 10 tasks left today, and breakfast is next.
Telling you something newFibre usually runs short by evening, and breakfast is the cheapest place to get ahead of it. The chilla option has 6 grams of the 30 you need today, so eat that one and log it.
The streak as a threatDo not break your 11 day streak now.
Gentle beats strictWalking after a meal does more for your glucose than walking before one. Take ten minutes after dinner and log the steps, and they count double.

She never recaps the day and she never names a coach in the line. Before anything is logged she asks for the first log rather than talking about a score that does not exist yet.

Honest list

What this still needs.

  • 1
    Real scores behind the four. Eat runs on nutrition sufficiency and Move on Momentum, which is worked out at /move. Mind and Measure are still counting tasks.
  • 2
    Which of the four nutrients is short. The Eat score already averages protein, carbs, fats and fibre, so the lowest of them is the biggest thing to fix. It turns "Eat 43" into "fibre is the gap".
  • 3
    What each planned meal is worth. The nutrition and the coach's options both exist, and nothing yet adds one up into "the chilla option carries 6 grams". That is the useful half of Kaira's best lines.
  • 4
    Knowing it is your first open today. Nothing knows whether this is the first visit of the day, so nothing can decide whether a greeting has earned its place.
