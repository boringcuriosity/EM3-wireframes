The Home screen

What do we say to you
every day, and every hour?

Somebody opens GoodFlip before breakfast, again at four o'clock, again after dinner. Each of those is a chance to say one true thing about that hour. Today the screen answers all three the same way, with a card about the program that has said the same sentence since the day they joined. This is the work to change that.

The insight

We built one half of the loop.

To-do works. It lists the day in the order it happens and a person works down it. What it gives us is adherence: the tasks get done. The half still missing is the reason to want to, and that is what the four pillars were always for.

To-do
Adherence

The day as a list. What is on it, when it happens, what is left. A person who works down it is doing the program.

EM3
Motivation

The four pillars as four scores. Where you stand, which one needs you right now, and what doing it is worth.

Every Home decision from here gets judged against this split. Something that helps a person do the task belongs to To-do. Something that makes them want to belongs to EM3.

What was in the way

The screen opened on a card about the year.

Your program, every time you open the app.

The program carousel took about a third of the first screen and said the same words in the morning as at midnight. It is real information and it belongs on Home, as background rather than as the opening line. It now sits below the day. The room it leaves at the top is where the four pillars go.

The concept

Four bubbles

One of them is bigger,
and the hour decides which.

  • 1
    The big one is the pillar that needs you now.
    It sits in the middle with its score and one short line about what to do.
  • 2
    The other three stay where they are.
    Mind is always top left, Move top right, Measure bottom right, Eat bottom left. Only the big one moves, so the screen looks the same every time you open it.
  • 3
    Tap any bubble to open that pillar.
    One tap, one meaning, whatever size it happens to be today.
  • 4
    Kaira sits underneath and joins them up.
    The scores say where you stand and the tasks say what to do. She is the only thing that can say which task moves which score.

Watch it move through a day on its own. The bubbles drift a little at rest, so the cluster feels alive rather than printed.

How far the score has to go

A score sitting at 35 has 65 to gain. A score at 80 has 20. The one further from full has more to win today, which is the whole reason a low score should be the one you see biggest.

What is waiting right now

Only the tasks open in the part of the day you are in, counted by what each one pays in Flipcoins. The coach's session pays 10, a meal pays 4, a nudge pays nothing. Those figures were set task by task across the whole app, so they already say how much each one matters.

One rule before all of it. A pillar can only take the middle if it actually has something waiting in this part of the day. That is what keeps the big bubble agreeing with the task list right underneath it.
When two are close

What breaks a tie.

  • 1
    More tasks waiting wins.
    A night with three Eat tasks is more of an Eat night than one with a single Move task.
  • 2
    Then whichever is due soonest.
    This comes last on purpose. At four o'clock the snack is sooner and the coach's session is the bigger thing, so the session takes the evening.
  • 3
    Then Eat, Move, Mind, Measure.
    The order the app already teaches. It means the answer is always the same on the same day, so the bubbles can never flicker between two pillars.
Play with it

Test it yourself.

Pick a moment in the day. The tasks waiting at that hour load with it, taken from a real planned day. Then move the scores and watch which bubble takes the middle.

Drag a score and the middle can change. Try dropping Move to 20 in the evening, or lifting Eat to 90 in the morning.

The line underneath

Being specific is what makes it warm.

A greeting that would work for any person on any day is what makes a line feel like a machine being friendly. Something only a system that was paying attention could say is warm on its own, and it tells you something at the same time. Kaira gets her warmth from three things and none of them is a pleasantry: she names the coach who chose it, she uses numbers that are only yours, and she assumes the best of you.

She always talks about the pillar in the big bubble, because a card whose two halves disagree is worse than a card that says less. A greeting earns its place about once a day, on the first open of the morning. By the afternoon it is a machine marking time.

How they behave

Alive, and worth meeting every hour.

At rest

A small drift

Four pixels over eight seconds, each bubble on its own timing so they never move together. Small enough that you never catch it moving, and enough that the cluster feels alive.

When it changes

The old one leaves first

Half a second, with the outgoing bubble stepping back before the new one comes forward. Both moving at once looks like a glitch. One after the other looks like it meant to. It happens about four times a day.

If you ask for less

Less motion, never a jump

With reduced motion on, the drift stops completely and the handover stays, just quicker. Taking the movement out entirely would replace it with a jump, which is harder to follow.

The bubble fills from the bottom to its own score, so four pillars can be compared at a glance without reading four numbers. That is what answers "is 62 any good" with nothing else on the screen.

Honest list

What this still needs.

  • 1
    Which of the four nutrients is short. The Eat score is already the average of protein, carbs, fats and fibre, so the lowest of the four is the biggest thing to fix. Two lines of code, and it turns "Eat 35" into "fibre is the gap". Nothing else on this list buys as much.
  • 2
    What each planned meal is worth. The nutrition and the coach's options both exist, and nothing yet adds one up into "Sahana's chilla option carries 6 grams". That is the useful half of most of Kaira's best lines.
  • 3
    Move's score needs two more inputs. The Momentum working is settled and lives at /move. It needs the coach's small daily nudges as real tasks, and movement recorded against the part of the day it happened in.
  • 4
    Mind's score needs a working. Sleep, the calm break and the psychologist's worksheets are all there. What they add up to is the piece to write next.
  • 5
    Knowing it is your first open today. Nothing knows whether this is the first visit of the day, so nothing can decide whether the greeting has earned its place.
  • 6
    A version for somebody with no coach yet. With no plan there are no real scores, and four empty bubbles is a worse answer than four pillars carrying today's work.
