From the nutrition team

Coach Insights by Dr. Vrushali

Five things every diet coach wants the user to understand.

01
Every plan is personal

The coach builds each plan around the person: their condition, lifestyle and goals. That includes the calories, protein, carbs and fats for every single meal.

02
Too much is harmful too

Eating more of any macro than planned is not healthy, protein included, which is a particular risk for kidney patients. Going more than 20% over is where it starts to matter.

03
Three macros matter

Protein, carbs and fats. Fibre is part of carbs and is already built into every plan, so it doesn't need to be tracked separately.

04
Timing is part of the plan

The coach decides what to eat and when. Eating within about 30 to 60 minutes of the planned time is fine, but following the timing is what makes the plan work.

05
Same calories isn't the same meal

Swapping a meal for another healthy one with similar macros is fine. Swapping it for junk is not. Calories alone don't equal sufficiency.

The maths, simply

How the score is worked out

1
Each meal has a target

It comes from the coach's planned items. For example, breakfast: 24 g protein, 31 g carbs, 18 g fats, worth 23 points of the day.

2
Each macro gets a mark out of 100
  • ShortThe share reached. Half the protein means 50.
  • WithinUp to 20% over is still 100. This is the coach's free buffer (at least 5 g).
  • OverLose 1 mark for every 1% over, but never below −50.
3
The meal's mark is the average of its three marks
4
The meal's points = its share × its mark

A breakfast exactly on plan earns 23 × 100% = 23.

5
Add up the six meals

That's the day's score, and it never shows below 0.

The principles behind it

  • Following the plan always scores 100.
  • Eating less costs points, but never more than that meal is worth.
  • Eating far too much can take back up to half of a meal's points.
  • A skipped meal scores 0 but counts as logged, so the day can finish.
  • Logging everything at night gives the same score as logging live.
  • Calories are shown, not scored, because they come from the three macros.
