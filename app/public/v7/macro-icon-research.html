Macro icons: what Indians read, what apps do, and what we could draw

The Eat screen shows three macros under the score: Protein, Carbs and Fats. Today each is a 3D glyph that fills with grey liquid. This document gathers what Indian users associate with each macro, how nutrition apps on Mobbin draw them, the principles that follow, and eight icon families drawn into the real card, each at 45%, 80% and 100%.

1. What Indian users picture

  • Protein means dal, then paneer. About 73% of urban Indians are protein deficient and 93% do not know their daily need; most believe dal, roti and chawal cover it. Dietitian content and the ICMR-NIN plate both lead with dal, paneer, chana, sprouts, curd and peanuts.
  • Carbs mean roti and chawal. The NIN "My Plate for the Day" (2024) groups them as cereals and millets, drawn as grains. For a diabetes-led audience, roti and rice are also the foods they are told to watch.
  • Fat is spoken of as ghee-tel. Hindi health media talk about ghee and oil, not an abstract nutrient; nuts are the positive fat cue. A plain drop reads as water.
  • Many users are vegetarian. NFHS-5: 23% of women and about 16% of men never eat meat or fish, rising to about half of women in north and central India. FSSAI classes egg as non-veg, and about 28% of women do not eat eggs.
  • The English words are the everyday words. Protein, carbohydrate and fat are used as loanwords in Hindi; "vasa" is textbook. Keep the English labels; let the icon say what food it is.
  • Never icon only. In a Dehradun study of 200 patients, 1% read all ten pharmacy pictograms unaided; locally designed pictograms far outperform imported ones, and for older users symbol plus text beats symbol alone.

Sources: ORF on IMRB and Right To Protein · Business Today · ThePrint · ICMR-NIN My Plate · India TV on ghee and oil · NFHS-5 via ThePrint · FSSAI veg and non-veg marks · Pictogram study, Dehradun · Symbols and text for older users. Indian apps (Swiggy Health Hub, Amul protein packs, Ultrahuman) show macros as numbers, bars and text badges; no Indian app with published macro icons was found.

2. What other products do

Seventeen products on Mobbin show macros. Most use no icon at all; the few that do agree on wheat for carbs and a drop for fat, and reach for meat for protein.

3. Principles for GoodFlip

Vegetarian firstNo meat, fish or egg as the default protein icon. Dal, paneer or chana carry protein for everyone.
Indian kitchen, not a western oneRoti, chawal and ghee over bread, pasta, avocado or butter.
Always with the wordIcon plus "Protein", "Carbs", "Fats". The icon identifies; the word confirms.
One colourThe macros share one family; the icon, not a hue, tells them apart. Works in greyscale.
Fits the systemGoodFlip icons are rounded 2px line icons at 24px, with filled active states.
Shows progressA line icon cannot fill, so it rides in a ring; a solid silhouette can fill like liquid.
Fat is gold, not waterIf fat is a drop, it reads as ghee only with a warm or spoon cue, never a plain blue drop.
Positive, not policingNo flame for fat (reads as calories) and no dumbbell for protein (feeds the "protein is for gym" myth).

4. Explorations

Every family is drawn twice where it can be: as a line icon inside a progress ring (the GoodFlip line style, and Cal AI's pattern), and, for the shapes that hold liquid, as a solid silhouette that fills the way today's glyphs do. Protein is at 43 of 95g, Carbs at 190 of 238g, Fats at target.

5. Recommendation

Lead with B, Indian kitchen: dal katori, roti and a ghee jar, as line icons in a progress ring. It is the only family that names each macro with the food Indian users already picture, it is vegetarian by default, it sits inside GoodFlip's line icon rules, and the ring shows progress without needing a silhouette to fill.

Keep D, the global set (bean, wheat, drop), as the fallback. It matches what every other app does for carbs and fats and is closest to today's glyphs; tint the drop gold if it ships, so it does not read as water.

Test before choosing. Five people in a tier 2 or 3 city, aged 40 and over, half vegetarian: show B and D without labels, ask which nutrient each icon is, then with labels. Pick the set read right most often unlabelled.
