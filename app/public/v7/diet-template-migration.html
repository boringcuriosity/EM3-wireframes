Old diet templates in the 7-meal builder

The old template editor let coaches add, rename and reorder meals. The new Create Diet Plan builder has 7 standard meals. This audit reads every live template, runs it through the new builder's own matching rules, and shows exactly where meals go wrong, then lays out ways to fix it for every coach.

25 Nov 2026 · Read-only audit of all 117 templates on coach.mytatva.in/diet-template, through the same API the portal uses · Matching rules read from MyTatva-Coach-React CreateDietPlanV2/lib/plan.ts and checked against the live production builder (diet-coach.mytatva.in, main.c47fa458.js) · Numbers re-verified 25 Nov
How the new builder places an old meal today
1. Same meal type id as a standard meal→2. Name contains "early morning", "bed time", "morning snack" or "mid", "evening", "breakfast", "lunch", "dinner"→3. Anything else goes to Lunch
Nothing is dropped. Every old meal lands in some standard meal, and its options are added as more options (alternatives) to whatever is already there. The meal's start time is overwritten by the last meal placed into it (to 00:00 if that meal had no time). Each meal's share of the day is set from the average of its options. Names are checked in that order, so any name with "mid" (Mid evening snacks, Mid night snacks) goes to Morning snack. A meal the coach hid in the template is placed like any other; the builder does not read hide_meal. Re-checked 25 Nov against planFromTemplate and slotKeyFor in the repo (master and development are the same here) and the live bundle, still main.c47fa458.js.

Two more risks, outside templates
  • Updating the template makes it permanent. When a coach assigns a plan and chooses to update the original template, the builder overwrites it with only the 7 standard meals (saveTemplate, kind "master"). The custom meals are gone and their foods stay wherever they were merged. Saving as a new template leaves the original untouched.
  • Patient plans. Edit and Copy use code that replaces options, but patient plans hold no custom meal names, so the template collisions do not repeat there. See the next section.

Copy and edit of patient plans

Corrected on 25 Nov: the copy risk is smaller than first reported
  • The code does replace. Edit and Copy of a patient plan load through planFromLegacyDiet, which replaces a meal's options instead of adding them. If two meals ever met in one standard meal, one would be lost.
  • But patient plans hold no custom meal names. A plan stores only a meal type from the fixed meal_types list (diet_plan_meal_rel has no name column), and the portal reads it with an inner join on that list. So "On wake up" or "Pre-workout" cannot exist in a patient plan, and the collisions found in templates do not happen on copy. An earlier figure of 46 lost meals came from running templates through the copy path and does not apply to real plans.
  • Not verified: what happened at assignment. A custom meal's type is not in meal_types, and plan meals are read through an inner join on that table. Whether such meals were saved and hidden, rejected, or converted when the old templates were assigned is unknown without reading real patient plans.

When a name matches two rows "Before" or "after" a meal wins over a single word, and it ignores minutes. So "before lunch" and "30 min before lunch" are both Morning snack, "post dinner" is Bedtime (not Dinner). Among single words, Bedtime and Early morning win, so "mid night snacks" is Bedtime and a bare "morning" is Early morning.

These are "as well as" items, so they need the add-on rule (solution 2).
Your question: add as other options, or append to each option? Append. In GoodFlip, options are alternatives: the patient eats one of them. "30 min before lunch: apple cider vinegar" or "Pre-workout: apple and black coffee" is not instead of a meal, it is as well as a meal. Adding it as another option lets the patient skip the real meal, and it drags the meal's target down. It should go into every option of the meal it joins, kept visible as its own small part ("Before breakfast, 7:30 AM: apple, black coffee"), and counted into that meal's calories.

Ways to fix it

Five ideas that solve different parts of the problem. They combine; the recommendation at the end picks a set.

1Placement Recommended

Keyword buckets, then the meal’s time, then ask

  • Use the time the coach set. Most misplaced meals have one (once awake at 5 to 7 AM, mid evening at 6 PM, bed - time at 11 PM); a few are unset at 00:00. A meal at 5 to 8 AM goes to Early morning, 6 PM to Evening snack, 10 PM or later to Bedtime.
  • When the time is unset (00:00), match a wider list of names: once awake, on wake up, on rise, on rising, morning, pre breakfast to Early morning; mid evening to Evening snack; mid night, post dinner to Bedtime.
  • Only if both fail, ask the coach instead of quietly using Lunch.
  • Never overwrite a standard meal's time with a merged meal's time.
FixesAll 35 misplaced meals and most of the 14 replaced times, for every future template. One function in the builder.

2Merge Recommended

Join as an add-on to every option

  • When an old meal lands in a standard meal that already has food, its foods are added to every option of that meal, marked as an add-on with its old name and time ("30 min before lunch, 1:00 PM").
  • When the standard meal is empty, the old meal simply becomes it (this is most cases once placement is fixed).
  • The meal's target includes the add-on, so it stays where the coach set it instead of falling.
  • In the builder the add-on shows once above the options and can be edited once; it is stored on each option so the patient app needs no change.
FixesThe 43 meals with extra options, their lowered targets and their replaced times. Patients can no longer pick a glass of water as lunch.
CostsThe add-on loses its own time on the patient's side unless the app shows the label. Storing it on each option duplicates foods in the data.

3Model

A timed companion inside a standard meal

  • Each of the 7 meals can carry up to two short "companions": a name, a time and foods, eaten as well as whichever option is picked ("Pre-workout 7:30 AM", "Post dinner 10 PM").
  • The patient app shows it as a small line in the meal card with its own tick; Nutrition sufficiency counts it with its meal.
  • Still 7 meals, so scoring, windows and the Eat screen stay as designed.
FixesEverything 2 fixes, keeps the coach's timing, and gives pre and post workout a proper home for the future.
CostsA data model change in the template, the patient plan and the app's meal card. Highest effort.

4Naming

Let coaches rename a standard meal for a plan

  • The meal type stays (Early morning), the label the patient sees can be the coach's ("Once awake", "On rising").
  • A coach who wants a pre-workout meal renames Morning snack "Pre-workout" for that patient.
  • Imported templates keep their old names as labels automatically.
FixesMost of what coaches lost: 31 of the 35 misplaced meals are just other words for Early morning or Bedtime.

5Rollout Recommended

Migrate once, and ask coaches to check the 41

  • Run the fixed conversion on all 117 templates once and save a 7-meal version beside the original (keep the original read-only, never overwrite it).
  • Mark the 41 templates "Check before use" in the template list, with who owns them.
  • On "Use template", a short note in the builder says what moved: "Once awake is now Early morning. Pre-workout was added to Breakfast." with Undo and Move to.
  • Read patient plans the same way before they are opened in the new builder.
FixesTrust. No coach finds a changed plan by surprise, and nothing is lost if the conversion is wrong.
CostsA one-time script, a flag on the list, and a small notice in the builder. Seven coaches to brief.

Recommendation

Ship 1, 2 and 5 now. Plan 3 and 4 next.
  1. Now: keyword buckets with time as the fallback (1); make Edit and Copy add options instead of replacing them, as a safety net; when a meal joins one that already has food, add it to every option as a labelled add-on, never as another option (2); migrate once beside the originals, flag the 41, and show what moved on "Use template" (5). Together these fix every case in this audit without changing the patient app.
  2. Next: meal labels per plan (4), so "Once awake" and "Pre-workout" read the way the coach wrote them; then companions (3) if coaches keep needing a second timed item around a meal.
  3. Before any of it: read a sample of real patient plans against their templates, to see whether custom meals were dropped when the old templates were assigned.
