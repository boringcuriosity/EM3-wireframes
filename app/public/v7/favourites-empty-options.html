Favourites, before there are any

Four ways to answer the same two questions on an empty tab: what is this for, and how does anything get in here. All four are live and looping, built from the logging screen's own row, heart and type, so what differs between two cards is only the idea.

Every one holds its finished state under prefers-reduced-motion, since the finished state is what the loop was teaching. The line under each is the copy that variant would ship with.

Built now

A. The shortlist

Three foods, hearted one after another. Says the tab is a shortlist of things you eat often, not a single dish you liked once.

Against it: three hearts filling on their own is the one thing that never happens, so it teaches the outcome more than the gesture.

B. One press, and what it leaves behind

A fingertip comes down on the heart, the heart takes, and a small heart lifts away towards the tab. One food, one gesture, one consequence.

For it: the only variant that shows a hand, which is what makes it read as an instruction rather than as decoration.

C. Into the tab

The row is hearted, then lifts and files itself into a Favourites pill above, whose count ticks up. Answers "where does it go" rather than "what do I press".

Against it: the pill is not a real thing on the screen, so it teaches a destination the user will then go looking for.

D. The heart fills up

Foods drop into an outlined heart, which fills red as they land. No rows, no press: a picture of the idea rather than of the interface.

Against it: it is the prettiest and the least instructive. Nothing in it tells a first-time user what to actually do.
