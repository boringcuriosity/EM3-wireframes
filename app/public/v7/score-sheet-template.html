The score sheet, as one template

One sheet for every log: one meal or many, up, the same or down, and any mix of protein, carbs and fats. The words come from a handful of fixed lines; only the meals, their macros and their points change. Every phone below is the same template with different data.

The template

1 · Top: a short title and a kind line

Every meal perfectYour sufficiency has increased
You’re doing great. Keep following your coach’s plan and staying within your macros.
Up, not every meal perfectYour sufficiency has increased
Good progress. Keeping each meal closer to your coach’s plan will help your score grow even more.
No changeYour sufficiency stayed the same
Try to keep each meal within your macros, and your score will improve a lot.
DownYour sufficiency has decreased
It happens sometimes, so don’t worry. Try to keep your macros in range for better nutrition.

The top of the sheet takes a soft tint: green up, grey the same, amber down.

2 · Each meal: two sentences and its macros

One sentenceHow it went, so what it did, against what it could have done.
How it wentYour {meal} was perfect · was bigger / smaller than planned. · had more {carbs and fats} than planned · had less {protein} than planned · had more {carbs} and less {protein} than planned
So it(perfect) Your {meal} was perfect and added {x}% to your sufficiency score. · (not all earned) only added {x}%. · didn’t change your sufficiency score. · took {x}% from your sufficiency score.
Example“Up by only 5%, could have gained 18% more” reads: Your breakfast was bigger than planned, so it only added 5%.
MacrosProtein, Carbs, Fats on a light grey tile, within target in dark: {eaten} / {target}g. The eaten number is amber when more, grey when less.

Within target is 80% to 120% of the macro target: a placeholder, to match the score’s own rule.
