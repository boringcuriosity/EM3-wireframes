Breathing: one player, or one for each exercise?

A coach can assign five breathing exercises, each with minutes and a time of day. This doc studies how each one should play on the Mind screen: what the reflection app does today, what the techniques actually ask of the body, what other apps do, and four live ways to build it. Every frame below is the real Mind screen playing the variant, with all five exercises at their real timings.

The principle at stake. Breathing is the one task in the plan the person does with their body, often with their eyes half closed. The screen has to lead the breath, then get out of the way. Consistency matters for the controls they touch (Start, Pause, End). The picture they follow can differ, because tracing a star and holding for seven counts are different jobs.

1The problem

Who

Adults in India managing diabetes, weight or blood pressure, most between 30 and 60. Breathing exercises are new to many of them. Some have tried pranayama and know "hold" and "slow out" from yoga class, which helps with 4-7-8 and Box.

When

  • After lunch at a desk, or in a short break at work.
  • Before a stressful moment: a meeting, a sugar test, a doctor visit.
  • In bed with the lights low (Sleep Breathing is made for this).
  • At the coach's time of day, nudged by a reminder.

State of mind

  • Often tense or tired, short of time, and a little unsure whether they are "doing it right".
  • Likely in company: sound may be off, so the phone must guide by sight and touch.
  • Interruptions are normal: a call, a child, a colleague.
  • Holding the breath can feel uncomfortable for people with high blood pressure or anxiety.

What they need from the screen

  • Know exactly what to do before starting, in one look.
  • While breathing, one thing to follow and one word to read.
  • Stop any time without losing what they did.
  • A clear "done" that counts towards their Rhythm score.

2Scenarios

Listed before designing. Every variant below handles all of them; the variant notes call out where one handles a scenario better.

The row on the Mind screen

  • Not started today, coach's time is later.
  • Coach's time has arrived (reminder brought them in).
  • Paused earlier, sheet closed mid-session.
  • Part done: some minutes saved, the rest still open.
  • Done for today.
  • Two or three exercises assigned on the same day.
  • No breathing in the plan (the row is absent).
  • The coach changes the exercise mid-day: the new one shows, saved minutes stay.

Ready (before starting)

  • First time ever with this exercise.
  • Done it before: wants to start fast.
  • Holds feel too long: a shorter count (Sleep and Box).
  • In public: sound off, buzz on.
  • Eyes closed preferred (Sleep, 5 Finger).
  • Changes their mind and closes the sheet.

Running

  • Phase changes: in, hold, out, rest.
  • Loses their place and needs the next step.
  • Feels light-headed during a hold.
  • Phone call or app goes to the background.
  • Screen locks or dims.
  • Closes the sheet with the X.
  • Time is up mid-breath: finish the breath out.

Paused and ending early

  • Paused, then resumes where they were.
  • Ends under a minute: too short to save.
  • Ends after a minute or more: save those minutes.
  • Comes back later the same day to finish the rest.
  • Leaves it paused overnight: the next day starts fresh.

Done

  • Finishes the coach's minutes: saved straight away.
  • Answers "How do you feel now?" or skips it.
  • Closes with the X on the done screen: still saved.
  • Rhythm score moves by the breathing share.
  • Wants to do it again: extra minutes are welcome and change nothing.

Access and edge cases

  • Reduced motion is on.
  • Screen reader: each phase is announced once.
  • Large text: phase word and controls still fit.
  • Offline: the session plays and saves later.
  • Battery saver blocks haptics: the visual still leads.

3The reflection app today

Studied two ways, read only. In Chrome, the public home and the demo account's Templates page (it shows the Zen Flow card; I did not press Start, because starting writes a session row to the app's database). The other four exercises are documented from the app's own source code in mind-lovable/src/components/modules/, which is the same app. Each is a separate card with its own colour, one shared timing engine (useBreathingEngine) and the same control row.

Indigo card Sleep Breathing

What you see. Title "Sleep-Inducing Breathing Technique". Three circles in a row reading 4s, 7s, 8s (Inhale, Hold, Exhale) joined by arrows. Below, a soft indigo bubble with "Ready" in it. When running, the bubble grows over 4 s, holds big for 7 s, shrinks over 8 s, with a big countdown number inside, a thin bar filling per phase and "Completed cycles". The active step circle gets a ring. Footer: "Repeat this cycle 3-4 times before sleep for best results."

Timing. 4 in, 7 hold, 8 out. 19 s a cycle. Runs until Stop.

The three step circles: the pattern in one look. Three progress signals at once (step ring, countdown, bar). The footer says 3 to 4 cycles, yet nothing stops or rests after four.

Green card Zen Flow Breathing

What you see (seen live). A pale green circle with "Ready" in it, inside a faint outer ring. Under it a teal "Start Exercise" button with a speaker icon beside it. Then four numbered steps: Breathe In, Breathe Out (repeat a calming word), Stay Present, Repeat for 1 to 5 minutes. Below a rule, "Calming words to use" with three chips: Peace, Relax, Let go.

Timing. 4 in, 6 out. 6 breaths a minute.

The calming word. It is the whole point of this exercise. The word never appears during the breathing; it sits in chips below the fold.

Amber card Star Breathing

What you see. Three written steps (trace up and breathe in, hold at the point, breathe out down the other side). A five-point star outline in amber with small labels around it: "Breathe in" at the top tip, "Hold" at the side tips, "Breathe out" at the bottom right. A dot travels the outline. Under it, "Breathe in, 4s" style text and "Completed stars". A colour legend (amber in, purple hold, blue out).

Timing. Each point: 4 s down, 4 s up, 2 s hold at the tip. 50 s a star.

A path to follow is the right idea for tracing. The dot starts at the top tip and goes down first, so the session opens on a breath out. The colour legend does not match the path, which is all amber. Labels sit at only some tips.

Teal card 5 Finger Breathing

What you see. Title "5 Finger Breathing, Calm Brain Break". A spiky hand outline (straight lines, pointed fingertips) with a teal dot. Text like "Breathe in, Thumb, 3s". Below the controls, a stock photo of a hand and five written steps ending "Continue across all five fingers, then reverse."

Timing. 4 s up each finger, 4 s down. 40 s a hand.

Naming the finger: it tells you where you are. The steps say reverse, the dot jumps from the little finger back to the thumb. Pointed fingertips do not look like a hand. The photo repeats what the outline already shows.

Teal card Box Breathing

What you see. A pale square outline with "Ready" in the middle. When running, a green dot travels the edges with a big countdown in the centre and the phase word above it. Footer: "Use anytime you need a reset".

Timing. 4 in, 4 hold, 4 out, 4 hold. 16 s a box.

Four sides, four steps: the shape is the pattern. The dot starts top left and travels along the top while you breathe in, so "in" moves sideways and "out" moves along the bottom. Up for in and down for out is the natural map.

Shared by all five

  • No length: the session runs until Stop, so the coach's minutes cannot be honoured.
  • Stop is a red, destructive button, and it resets to zero. There is no pause.
  • Nothing marks a session as done or counted.
  • Sound on by default: soft rising tone on in, falling tone on out, a low tick on hold. No haptics.
  • No reduced-motion handling; the countdown changes every second throughout.
  • Five colour themes for one kind of task.

7Variants

Each frame is the real Mind screen (/v7/mind/index.html) drawing the variant from variants/breath.js. Pick the exercise, the state, the coach's minutes, first time or done before, reduced motion, and a faster speed for review; the frame reloads in that state. Everything inside is live: Start, Pause, End, the X (it pauses), and the row after you close. Timings are real at 1×. Haptics fire on Android through the vibration API; on a laptop they are silent.

8The row on the Mind screen, before and after

Today the breathing row borrows the tick circle from steps that tick in place, yet tapping it opens a player, and it describes the technique in the same words for every state. After: the exercise's own glyph leads the row (the same glyph the player uses), a thin ring around it fills with saved minutes, the title stays put, and only the line under it and the one button change.

9Haptics and sound

Haptics (on by default)

  • Breathe in: one light tap at the start; on iOS a soft continuous rise across the breath (Core Haptics), like Apple Watch Breathe.
  • Hold: two quick light taps at the start, nothing during.
  • Breathe out: one medium tap; on iOS a slow fade across the breath.
  • Rest (4-7-8): nothing; the quiet is the cue.
  • Done: three gentle taps.
  • Off when the phone is on silent? Apple keeps haptics on silent; so should we. A setting turns them off.

Sound (off by default, remembered)

  • Soft sine tones, as the reflection app has: rising on in, a low single tone on hold, falling on out. Volume follows the phone.
  • Sleep Breathing at night: tones one step lower and quieter.
  • Later: a recorded voice in Hindi and English for first-timers ("Breathe in through your nose").
  • Default off because most sessions happen in company; variant D asks for sound when the guide fades.

Screen and system

  • Keep the screen awake while running (Wake Lock).
  • App to background, call, lock: pause at that second, save nothing yet.
  • Screen reader: announce the phase word once per phase, never the countdown.
  • Reduced motion: the orb stops scaling and brightens and dims instead; the path draws in one-second steps with no moving dot.

10Recommendation

B, one shell with a guide made for each exercise. The person learns one set of controls (Start, Pause, End, the same session bar) and one way of ending early, so a second or third exercise feels familiar. Inside that shell each exercise gets the picture its technique needs: a hold ring and count for 4-7-8, the calming word for Zen Flow, a star and a hand to trace, four sides for Box. It fixes every problem found in the reflection app at the cost of five small drawings inside one screen.

Take two things from the others. From C, the night look for Sleep Breathing after 9 PM, because a bright sheet in a dark bedroom works against the exercise. From D, the settle mode as an option the person turns on, for Sleep and 5 Finger first, where eyes-off is the goal.

The catch: five visuals means five things to test with reduced motion, large text and a screen reader. The engine stays one, which keeps timings honest.

Finishing early, in all variants

Minutes add up through the day. A session of a minute or more saves its whole minutes, and the row shows "2 of 5 min" with Finish to do the rest later. The breathing share of the score (10 points) fills in proportion; on the prototype screen only a full session counts for now, since the score reads breathing as done or not done. Under a minute is too short to save. When the time is up mid-breath, the guide finishes the breath out before ending, so nobody is cut off holding.

11Open questions

1. What counts when someone ends early?

  • Minutes add up (recommended). Save whole minutes, score in proportion, finish later. Honest and kind.
  • Any session counts in full. Simplest, but two seconds earns the same as fifteen minutes.
  • Half or more counts in full. One rule to explain; anything under half is lost.
  • Only the full time counts. Clear, but punishes interruptions.

2. 4-7-8 longer than a few minutes

  • Rounds of 4 with rests (recommended). Follows Dr Weil; 5 min is about 3 rounds.
  • Cap at 4 breaths in the coach portal. Sleep Breathing becomes a 2 minute item only.
  • Play straight through. As today; risks light-headedness for beginners.

3. Cyclic sighing

  • Add it as a sixth exercise (recommended). Best evidence for daily mood; easy shared-shell visual (two short climbs, one long fall).
  • Replace Zen Flow with it. Keeps five; loses the calming word.
  • Leave the five as they are. Change plan.js's sample to one of the five.

4. Night look for Sleep Breathing

  • Dark sheet after 9 PM (recommended). Kind to eyes in bed; a deliberate exception to white.
  • Always dark for Sleep Breathing. Consistent for that exercise; odd at 2 PM.
  • Always white. Follows the white rule; bright in a dark room.

5. Calming words for Zen Flow

  • Peace, Relax, Let go, plus Shanti and Sukoon (recommended). Familiar words in Hindi and Urdu feel personal.
  • Peace, Relax, Let go only. As the reflection app.
  • Let the person type their own. Most personal; more to design.

6. Settle mode (from D)

  • Off by default, offered on Sleep and 5 Finger (recommended).
  • On for everyone after the third session. More eyes-off practice; some will think the screen froze.
  • Leave it out. Simplest.
