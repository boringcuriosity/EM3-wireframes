Last night's sleep on the Mind screen

How last night shows, where it sits, and how a patient enters or fixes it. Sleep is 47 of the 100 Rhythm points: hours 23 and timing 24, both measured against the bedtime, wake time and hours the coach sets. Times come from Apple Health or Health Connect when they are connected, and the patient can always enter or edit them. Each variant below runs on the real Mind screen (the coach portal's preview, with this variant switched on). Pick a state above it, tap the card, change the times, save.

1. Where it sitsIts own "Last night" card above the plan, like steps. Tonight's bedtime stays a row in the Night part.
2. Best visualHours as the hero number, with the night drawn as a bar over the coach's window and a legend for last night and the plan (J).
3. Entering and editingA sheet whose bar is dragged by its ends, two time fields with a scroll wheel for exact minutes, and "Add another sleep" for a split night. Midnight is worked out for them.
4. PointsTargets live in "What shaped your score?" above the card: Sleep hours, Sleep window ("On time" or "45 min late") and Self-care.

The problem

Who and when. A patient with a metabolic condition opens Mind in the morning, often still in bed or over the first chai, somewhere between 6 and 9 AM. They want one thing: did last night count, and is it right? Most open the screen on a phone without a watch, so for many of them nothing fills in by itself (see the platform notes in sleep-data-health-platforms: sleep needs a watch or another app that writes it).

State of mind. Groggy and short on patience. If the night was poor they may feel a little guilty already. If the numbers look wrong (a watch taken off at 4 AM shows two hours) they feel judged by a machine. So the card has to be readable at a glance, easy to correct in a few taps, and kind about a bad night.

What the coach set. In bed by 10:30 PM, up at 6:00 AM, 7h 30m of sleep. Full hours points at 7h 30m, dropping to zero when 3 hours short. Full timing points when both times fall within 30 minutes of the plan, dropping to zero at 2 hours away.

Scenarios

Listed before designing, grouped by what they belong to. Every one of these is either a state in the phones below or a rule in the sheet.

Where the data comes from

  1. Health is connected and last night came through.
  2. Health is connected but has nothing for last night (no watch, watch not worn, watch flat).
  3. Health is connected but last night has not arrived yet (the watch has not synced, or Health Connect writes the night only once it ends).
  4. Health is not connected: permission was never asked, or was turned down.
  5. The patient turned off sleep in Health's own settings after connecting.
  6. Two sources write sleep (a watch and a sleep app): use what Health lists as the night, never add them up.

What last night looks like

  1. A normal night inside or near the window.
  2. A night with a wake-up in the middle (awake 20 minutes at 3 AM).
  3. A very short night that is real (a late flight).
  4. A very short night that is wrong (watch taken off, only 2h 05m came through).
  5. Asleep after midnight (bed at 1:15 AM is still last night).
  6. A long lie-in past the coach's hours plus one.
  7. Night shift sleep in the day: out of scope here, flagged for the coach.

Entering and editing

  1. Entering last night with nothing synced.
  2. Fixing a synced night (the card then says it was edited by you).
  3. Going back to Health's times after an edit.
  4. Woke up a moment ago: one tap sets the wake time to now.
  5. Slept as planned: one tap fills the coach's times.
  6. Closing the sheet without saving keeps the card as it was.
  7. Health sends new data after a manual entry: the patient's times stay.

Times that need a check

  1. Wake-up before fell asleep.
  2. Wake-up later than now.
  3. A night over 14 hours (usually AM and PM swapped).
  4. A night under 3 hours (allowed, with a gentle check).
  5. Crossing midnight, worked out from the clock: an evening time is yesterday, a morning time is today.

The score

  1. Before last night is in, the sleep part of the score waits; the rest of the day still counts.
  2. Hours and timing count separately: a full night at the wrong time still earns its hours.
  3. Points move as soon as times are saved, and move back if an edit is undone.
  4. The patient never sees a minus.

Plan and day

  1. No coach plan yet: last night still shows, with no window drawn and no score.
  2. The coach changed the window yesterday: last night is measured against the plan that was live last night.
  3. Looking at an earlier day: that night's card, read only after 7 days.
  4. Opening Mind in the evening: the card still shows last night; tonight's bedtime is in the Night part.

Where sleep sits

Sleep is different from everything else on the Mind plan: it already happened, it is mostly measured by the phone, and it is half of the score. Steps on Move are the nearest match: they come from Health, can be entered by hand, and have their own card ("Your steps", under the plan, with a source strip at its foot). A split night

Some nights go: asleep, awake for a while, asleep again. Most people only ever enter a bedtime and a wake time, so that stays the default. A split night is one quiet link away.

PartHow it works
Entering itUnder the two fields sits "+ Add another sleep", with "Woke in the night and slept again?" under it. A tap splits the last sleep in two around its middle, 40 minutes apart, so both pairs start close to right and only need a nudge. The pairs get small "Sleep 1" and "Sleep 2" labels; the wheel opens under the pair in use. At most 3 sleeps.
Taking one away"Remove" on Sleep 2 or 3 joins it back into the sleep before, the exact reverse of adding, so nothing is lost by a mis-tap. Sleep 1 has no Remove.
Why this wayChosen over a separate "awake from, to" field (one more idea to learn) and over a second sheet (leaves the place the person is in). The pair is the same control they already used, repeated.
On the cardThe bar shows two indigo segments with a hatched gap between them. The hours are time asleep only ("6h 40m of sleep, 50 min short"). The awake time is noted once, on the times line: "10:50 PM to 6:10 AM, awake 40 min at 2:30 AM, on time".
TimingThe first bedtime and the last wake, against the coach's window, as for any night.
From HealthHealth Connect sends each session as its own record that cannot overlap; Apple Health sends asleep samples with gaps. Sessions that start between 6 PM and noon and sit less than 3 hours apart become one night with a gap. A longer gap, or a sleep in the day, is a nap.
ChecksEach sleep in order ("Sleep 2: woke up has to come after fell asleep"). No overlaps ("Sleep 2 starts before sleep 1 ends. Move it later"). Last wake before now. Under 14 hours from first to last ("Check AM and PM"). The field at fault gets the red edge.

What others do. Oura shows awake time as gaps in the night's bar, and treats its longest period as the night without adding shorter ones (Oura, Sleep Graphs). Fitbit merges two sleeps less than an hour apart by itself, and otherwise asks people to delete and re-edit logs to join them (Fitbit Community). That is the clumsy path we avoid with Add and Remove. Rise lets people edit when they fell asleep and woke, then recalculates the night (Rise).

Indigo and teal, and why each goes where it does

Indigo is the night itself: the hours number and the bar of last night. Sleep happens in the dark, and indigo is already the colour of the sleep bar in A and in the GoodFlip ring file. Teal is the coach's window and "on time": teal is Mind's score colour, and the window is what earns the timing points, so a bar sitting inside teal reads as "this counted". Green stays for actions (Edit, Enter Times), as everywhere in the app.

Tokens used, all from the GoodFlip design system (color.indigo, color.teal, color.semantic): indigo 25 #F5F8FF, 50 #EEF4FF, 100 #E0EAFF (border-indigo-subtle), 200 #C7D7FE, 500 #6172F3, 600 #444CE7, 700 #3538CD, 800 #2D31A6, 900 #2D3282; teal 50 #EEFFFF, 100 #E0FFFF, 200 #C7FEFE (border-teal), 300 #A4FDFD, 900 #2D8282; warning 700 #B54708 for the odd-data line. Contrast: teal 900 text on white 4.54:1; teal 300 on indigo 900 9.55:1; indigo 200 on indigo 900 7.71:1; white on indigo 900 11.1:1; indigo 700 on white 8.1:1. Teal 800 (#2DA6A6) is only 2.95:1 on white, so it is never used for text or edges.

Showing the points without a points chase

The worry: if the card says "+21 points", people will learn to edit times up to 7h 30m. Three ways to show it:

Words on the card, numbers one tap away Recommended

Hours tile: "7h 05m, of your 7h 30m". Timing tile: "On time" or "1h 40m late". The info button opens "How last night counts" with Hours 20 of 23 and Timing 24 of 24. The Rhythm score above moves on save, which is the reward.

Numbers on the card

"Hours 20/23 · Timing 24/24" on the tiles. Honest and quick, but it puts the score in the patient's eyeline every morning and invites topping it up with edits.

Only the Rhythm score

No sleep numbers anywhere; the score just changes. Calmest, but the patient cannot tell whether hours or timing held them back, so they cannot act on it.

Recommendation

J, in White or Night scene. It builds on I with your edits: each fact once, the targets in "What shaped your score?", and a sheet whose bar can be dragged. I stays below as it was.

  • It is A, the card you liked: same number, same bar over the coach's window, same times, same Edit, same source strip.
  • Nothing is lost. The tiles' two meanings come back as words in A's own lines: "10 min short" after the hours, "on time" or "bed 1h 40m late" after the times.
  • A split night fits without a new screen: "+ Add another sleep" in the same sheet, two segments on the same bar, the awake time noted once.
  • Skin: soft indigo is my pick. It keeps the white page, and the teal window ties sleep to the Mind score. Deep night if you want sleep to stand apart.

Open questions

1. Do naps count toward hours?

Decided, 2 Nov: no separate nap case. A second sleep is added as a split night in the same sheet.

2. Should an edit to synced data count in full?

  1. Yes, and the coach sees "edited" (recommended). Trusts the patient, keeps a light check.
  2. Yes, but only edits made the same morning; older nights stay as synced.
  3. Synced times are locked; only an empty night can be entered.

3. Time in bed or time asleep for hours?

  1. Asleep, minus awake gaps, when Health has them (recommended). Matches what the coach means by hours of sleep.
  2. Time in bed, the same for synced and entered nights.

4. Show a week of nights?

  1. Not on the card; a 7-night strip inside "How last night counts" (recommended). Timing is about steadiness, and a week shows it best.
  2. A small 7-night strip under the bar on the card.
  3. Leave it to the Trend tab.

5. How far back can a patient enter or edit?

  1. Last night, plus the 6 nights before from the date picker (recommended).
  2. Last night only.
  3. Any night since joining.

6. Card placement against Move.

  1. Mind puts last night above the plan; Move keeps steps under it (recommended). Each sits where it is needed in the day.
  2. Move it under the plan to match Move.
