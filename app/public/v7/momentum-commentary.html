The line under the score

Written by the app, not the AI, from a few fixed sentences. Each one notices the effort first, then gives one easy next step in everyday words. Only the % and the situation change. The detail lives in the note behind Learn more.

How the app picks

First100% reached, then the medal (crossed 80%), then already above 80%
ThenMore than the coach’s workout: all at once if it went from under half to beyond the plan in one log
ThenFirst log of the day, then steps only, then any other log
Adds nothingOnly after a workout log when the workout’s calories are already met. A log that adds under a point leaves the line as it was
TogetherChanges within the line’s 2.4 s wait are said as one gain, from where the first began
NeverA minus. Logging never lowers Momentum.

Every note, in one place

What KAIRA wrote for each case, drawn by the same sheet the app opens. KAIRA sends markdown only: a bold opening, a short paragraph, What helps next as points, a closing line. The sheet turns the opening into a title, each point onto KAIRA’s gold turn-arrow, a gain into a green tag.

How these notes were tested

  • Real runs. Claude Haiku 4.5, one per case, on the facts the live screen produces. The prompt’s own examples use different numbers, so nothing here is copied.
  • Checked like the app would. Every percentage is one the app sent; every change has its sign and %; every “from to” range starts at today’s score; no minus, no dash inside a sentence, no jargon (kcal, points, “parts of the day”); only bold and “- ” points, at most two; under 95 words.
  • What the first rounds caught, now fixed in the prompt. KAIRA guessed why a score moved (now the app sends where the change came from), credited the medal to the wrong log (now the app sends the medal state), said one big effort beats moving often (now forbidden), and in the points round wrote “Rest is part of getting better” and grand closings like “changed the day entirely” (both now ruled out).
  • Retry, then fall back. If a reply fails a check, ask once more; if it fails again, show the app’s own template note. In this round Half of each routine passed on the retry (the first try named a score the app never sent). A run instead of the routine and Gym instead of the plan passed the checks but read oddly, so they were run again after the prompt fix.
