Steps, every case

Today a person can log steps by hand on Move, several times in one go, each at its own time. Their phone may also be sending steps from Apple Health or Health Connect. This page works out what happens when the two meet: can a log be seen, changed or removed, which number counts when both cover the same hour, and what the person, the score and the coach see in every state the connection can be in.

The problem

Steps are 25 of the 100 Momentum points, capped at the coach's goal (10,000 by default). Most people will connect their phone and never think about steps again. A smaller group logs by hand because the phone missed real walking: it sat on a desk during a treadmill walk, it was charging, it was in a bag on a pram, or they have no wearable and forget the phone at home. The research backs this up: iPhones undercount by about 900 steps a day for people who almost always carry them and about 3,000 for people who seldom do. A manual log is a correction to a count the phone got wrong, and the screen should treat it as one. That means three things. Logs must be visible and fixable. They must sit beside the phone's count in a way that never double counts by surprise. And the coach must be able to tell the two apart, because self-reported activity runs higher than measured activity.

What the prototype does today

  • Log steps on the card opens a sheet with rows of Steps and Around (a time). Add another time adds a row, up to 6. Save says Save 2 logs and so on. Up to 5 digits per row (99,999). A time later than now is blocked: That's later than now. Pick a time up to 3:40 PM.
  • Each row is added on top of whatever the hour already had. Logging 1,200 and 800 at 3:40 PM over Apple Health's 230 made that bar read 2,230 steps, 3 PM to 4 PM, so far, and the total went from 5,080 to 7,080.
  • After a log, the strip still reads From Apple Health, Updated 2 min ago. The card, the bar tip and the strip give no sign that 2,000 of those steps were typed in.
  • A saved log cannot be seen as a log, changed or removed. Tapping a bar shows only the hour's total.
  • With ?health=off, connecting adds Health's whole day on top of anything already logged, so a walk logged by hand and also counted by the phone counts twice.
  • Logged workouts already have a menu with Edit and Remove (it says the kcal will leave the score). Step logs have nothing like it yet.

    Apple Health and Health Connect, case by case

    A plain walk through what each platform does in every situation that touches manual steps, and how much room GoodFlip has in each. Read this part on its own if you only want the platform picture.

    How much freedom we have

    • Fully ours: how logs are shown, edited and removed; how a log combines with the phone's count inside GoodFlip; limits; what counts for the score; what the coach sees.
    • Shared with the platform: the phone's own total. We read it through the platform's merge, which already follows the order the person set in the Health app or Health Connect. We can read each source on its own if we ever need to show it.
    • Outside our reach: another app's steps. Both platforms let an app delete only what it wrote itself. A wrong count from the phone or a watch can only be fixed in Apple Health or Health Connect, by the person.
    • Invisible to us on iPhone: whether reading steps was refused. Apple shows a refused read as no data, so a day of zero steps and a refused permission look the same.

    What others do

    The pattern: apps built around their own device (Samsung Health, Garmin, Whoop, StepsApp, Noom) give no way to type steps. Apps built around logging (Apple Health, Google Fit, Pacer, Lose It!, Fitbit) allow it. Every app that allows it marks the log as manual, and challenge or reward features leave manual steps out.

    Take

    • Apple Health's Show All Data: every entry as a row with its time and a Delete. The simplest honest view of what was typed in.
    • Pacer's limit of 30,000 steps per entry, and its rule that a team admin can see each person's manual steps. Close to our coach.
    • Apple's order of sources, with manual entries first, tells us people expect a typed correction to be respected.
    • Health Connect's per-entry trash icon and delete by time range: removal is normal and expected.

    Avoid

    • Garmin and Noom send people off to another app to add steps. It reads as a dead end for a person who walked without their phone.
    • Pacer has no edit, so a typo means delete and log again. Two steps for a one-digit fix.
    • Whoop's lack of any correction is a standing complaint from treadmill-desk users.
    • Apple Health's priority list asks a person to understand data sources. Our people should never need to.
