Move: your plan

The meal cards go. In their place: Your plan, then the workout plan's name and dates, then the routine chips, then one card per exercise with its video thumbnail. Every phone below is live: tick, log and switch routines. The sheet that a name or info icon opens is shown at the end.

Data is what the backend sends today (live plan "Others plan", 14 to 27 Oct, Sahana Physio): name, video and its length, exercise type, reps, sets, rest after a set, rest after the exercise, description, steps, do's and don'ts, a done flag and a difficulty answer. Thumbnails are the real ones from the content library; three moves have their real videos (Box breathing, Plank to push up, Spot jump). Tap any thumbnail to play that move in the player further down. The extra detail lives behind each card's info door, in one sheet shown at the end.

1 · Video cards

The video leads each card behind a glass play button. Under it, four chips: sets, reps, rest between sets, rest before the next exercise. Two lines of what it's for, then "Mark as done"; once done, how it felt.

Trade-off: the richest per exercise and the closest to what users know. A long routine is a long scroll.

2 · Swipe through

Bigger cards in a row you swipe, framed like the video cards. Routine 1 and Routine 2 are two sessions stacked one after the other, not chips to choose between, because both are meant to be done. Each has its own row and its own start button.

Trade-off: the most compact and visual. Cards off screen are easy to miss.

3 · Routine cards

Each routine is a card like Eat's meal cards: its part of the day and minutes, the coach's moves to tick, "Did another workout?", and tips from the coach. A tick asks how it felt (easy or difficult) before it counts. Steps close the page: the day hour by hour, under the coach's parts of the day, with "Log steps".

Trade-off: the most familiar, since it is Eat's card. The hourly trend needs hourly data from the phone's health app; today the backend keeps one total a day.

Behind the info door

Shared by all four. Tapping a card's name or info icon opens this sheet, so cards carry only what you need to start. Title top left, circle X top right, no handle, never above 90%.

  • On the card: name, thumbnail, minutes, sets and reps, the tick.
  • In the sheet: the video, sets, reps, rest after a set, rest after the exercise, exercise type, the steps, and do's and don'ts.
  • Gone: "Read More" and the long inline text, the "Exercise Type:" label row, the separate Done pill beside the title.

Playing the routine

Each video is a short lesson, under a minute. It plays once with sound and stops on "Watch again". Every video control lives on the video, YouTube style, and the progress bar can be dragged. Below it, only what you need: the coach's numbers, what's next, and one button. Try it, or jump to a case.

Learning the exercise

Tap a thumbnailThe clip plays once from the start, logo and all, with sound. A thin green line along the bottom shows how far it is.
Tap the videoThe controls appear for 3 seconds: previous, play or pause, next, the time and full screen. Drag the bar, or its dot, to any point in the clip.
The clip endsIt stops on a still with "Watch again". No loop: a voice repeating while you count reps only gets in the way.
Turn the phone sidewaysOr tap the expand icon. The video fills the screen and "Next" stays in reach.
No video for this exercise yetThe still picture, a quiet label, and "Know more" for the steps.

Doing it

What to do, at a glanceUnder the name: Sets, Reps and Rest as three tiles, and one line for the rest before the next exercise. You count your own sets; the app doesn't make you tap through them.
Know moreThe info link opens the steps, do's and don'ts, and what the exercise is for.
"I'll do it later"The next button on the video. The exercise goes to the end of the routine with "Moved to the end · Undo". Never counted as done.
The whole routineA sticky bar at the foot joins "Up next" to the one "Next" button. "See all" opens the list: done (with how it felt), now, later, still to do. Tap any to jump.

After each exercise

Next, then restThe coach's 15 seconds count down and ask "How was it? Easy · Difficult". The answer is needed; give it during the rest and nothing is held up.
Rest over, no answer yetThe clock stops at 0:00: "Pick one to start Spot jump". One tap and the next exercise starts with its video.
The last exerciseNo rest to count. The same question, then the summary.

The endActive minutes, each exercise with how it felt, and anything left for later marked as still on today's plan. Then Eat's win.

Starting

"Start routine", or the first cardPlays the first move not yet done. A routine half done opens where you left it, never at the top.
Tapping a later cardPlays that move. The ones before it stay waiting in the list. Nothing is skipped just because you chose to start further down.
Tapping a move already donePlays it to watch again. Its sets show done, the button says "Watch again" and nothing is counted twice.
Routine 2, or a past daySame player with its own list and its own ticks. On a past day the moves count toward that day.

Doing it

Watch, then do your setsThe demo loops while you work. One big button for the set you are on: "Set 1 done". The set marks are large enough to read from the floor.
Rest between setsThe coach's 30 seconds counts down with "+15 sec" and "Skip rest". The video dims so the clock leads. Set 2 starts by itself.
Rest before the next moveThe coach's 15 seconds, with the next move's picture and name, so you can get into position. It plays by itself when the rest ends.
Play the next one by itself, offFor someone who wants to breathe. The rest ends and waits on "Start Spot jump". The switch lives in the list sheet.
Watching it biggerThe expand icon on the video, or simply turning the phone sideways, fills the screen. Only what matters from the mat stays: out, play or pause, the time, and the set button. Rests show over the picture.
Pausing from the matTap anywhere on the video. The big play sits where the eye already is, and the 60px pause below is reachable with a knee or knuckle.

Moving around

Next and previousNext skips the move: it stays unticked and shows as skipped, never as done. Previous goes back without undoing anything.
Jump anywhereThe list button opens the routine as a sheet: done, now, skipped, still to do. Tap any move to play it.
Skip a rest, add time"Skip rest" starts the next set or move at once. "+15 sec" adds time and can be tapped again.

Interruptions

A call, the app in the background, the screen lockingPlayback pauses and comes back paused on the same set, never further on. The screen stays awake while a move is playing.
Leaving halfwayThe X asks once, and says what is already safe: every finished move is saved the moment its last set ends. "Keep going" is the main button.
Coming back laterThe plan card points at the next move ("Up next" in option C), so the player opens there.

When something goes wrong

The video won't play, or no networkSays so on the video with "Try again" and "Read the steps". The sets still work, so a dead connection never blocks the workout.
A move with no videoSide leg raise has a picture but no video yet. The still shows with a quiet label, "How to" carries the steps, and the sets and rests work the same.

Ending

Everything doneWhat you did, in minutes, and one question the coach already uses: easy or hard. Then the same win as Eat: green sweep, the pill, the gain, one line.
Some moves skippedThe summary marks them and says they are still on your plan. No "3 of 4", just what was done and what is waiting.
The winPlays once after "Done", then hands back to Move with the score stepping up.

Patterns drawn from Mobbin: rest with a countdown and the next move (Ladder, Hevy, Google Health), a player with the overview below (Centr), the routine as a list sheet (Peloton Strength+, Future), pause with Resume and End (Peloton Strength+), autoplay as a switch (Vimeo), a one-question finish (stoic.). Heuristics leaned on: status always visible (sets, segments, rest clock), user control (skip, back, jump, leave with nothing lost), error recovery (video fails, workout doesn't), recognition over recall (the next move is shown, not named).
