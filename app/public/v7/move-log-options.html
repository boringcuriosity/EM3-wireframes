Move · logging a workout

Logging a workout in as few taps as a thought

Someone walked after lunch. How do they tell GoodFlip in the fewest taps, with no doubt about what happens, when many are 35 to 60, live with diabetes or PCOS, and are not fitness people? We searched Mobbin 56 times across about 20 apps, then built every serious option so you can try it here, with a tap counter on each phone.

Today's flow is the baseline: a search list, then a "How long?" screen with a minus and plus stepper, and Effort and When rows that each open another sheet.

01What the market does

Three things almost every good app agrees on, and one thing nobody does yet.

30 min
The form arrives already filled in

Oura opens on now, 30 min and Moderate. Fitbit also starts at 30. The common case becomes one tap on Save, and the user only edits what is different.

Oura, Add an activity · Fitbit, Add exercise
Chips
Presets beat wheels for duration

Cal AI uses 15 / 30 / 60 / 90 chips, Apple Fitness 5 to 45, Finch eight large tiles. Hour, minute and second wheels (Nike Run Club, Garmin) ask for a precision nobody remembers.

Cal AI · Apple Fitness · Finch
3 words
Effort in body words, not numbers

Cal AI says "Breaking a sweat", Bevel "heavy breathing, hard to talk". Apps with a 1 to 10 scale still need a sentence to explain it, so the sentence is the real control.

Cal AI · Bevel · Gentler Streak
2 taps
Nobody makes the repeat fast and the first log short

Lifesum repeats in 2 taps from recents, and Runna ticks a planned workout in 1. No app we saw also keeps a first-time log to 3. That gap is the opportunity.

Lifesum · Runna

02How long: seven controls, one task

Each control starts at 30 min. Try to set 45 minutes and watch the move count. The bars rate speed, precision, comfort for older or shaky thumbs, and how well a screen reader can work it.

03How hard was it

Effort changes the calories by up to a quarter, so it matters, but it is a feeling, not a number. The talk test (could you chat, talk, or only get a few words out?) is how clinicians check intensity and needs no explaining.

04When did it happen

Most logs happen right after the walk, so "Just now" should already be chosen. People remember the part of the day, not the minute, so the other choices are parts of the day, with an exact time one tap further for those who care.

05Six flows you can use

Every phone works. The counter shows taps until the workout is logged. Try the same four jobs on each: a 30 min walk, yesterday's walk again, the coach's plan, and 45 min of easy yoga this morning.

07Why flow A works, heuristic by heuristic

Nielsen's ten heuristics, Gestalt, and Hick's and Fitts's laws, applied to the recommended flow. The same lens produced the scores above.

H1Visibility of status

The calorie line updates as the duration changes, the button names what it will log ("Log walk"), and the Move totals rise the moment it lands.

H2Match the real world

"Just now", "This morning", "Could talk, not sing". The words people use when they tell a friend, not MET, RPE or hh:mm:ss.

H3Control and freedom

One-tap logs from recents show Undo for five seconds. "Change" in the sheet header goes back to the activity list without losing anything.

H4Consistency

One sheet pattern for the whole job, the same as Eat's: circle X top right, title top left, no handle, at most 90% of the screen.

H5Error prevention

No free typing for the common case, a range of 5 to 240 min, and no future times. The value is always a sensible one.

H6Recognition over recall

Recents remember duration and effort, the coach's plan is pinned on top, and eight pictured activities replace an A to Z list.

H7Flexibility

Chips for the usual, the stepper for in-between values, hold to move faster, and search for the forty other activities.

H8Minimal design

Three groups, each with one job. No kcal field, no end time, no notes. Calories are a result, never an input.

H9Recover from errors

A wrong log is undone from the toast, or opened again from the list, where the same sheet comes back filled in.

GestaltProximity and common region

The duration value, its chips and the calorie line sit in one region; effort in another; time in a third. The eye reads three questions, not twelve controls.

GestaltSimilarity and continuity

Every choice looks the same (a pill or card that fills green when chosen). The order runs what, how long, how hard, when: the order people recall it in.

GestaltFigure and ground

A sheet over the dimmed Move screen keeps the user where they were. Logging is a moment on top of their day, not a trip to another place.

HickFewer choices, faster picks

Six duration chips, three efforts, four times of day, eight tiles. Each group is small enough to take in at a glance.

FittsBig targets near the thumb

Every target is at least 44 pt, chips are 44 tall, and the main button is full width at the bottom of the sheet.

08Wheel first, one screen

How long goes in the middle of the screen as a big wheel, the way calm timer apps do it. Exercise, effort and when sit below as three cards, each opening a small sheet. Your last workout is already filled in, so a repeat takes one tap.

Wheel first Variation

The wheel is fine here because it is the only thing on screen and the numbers are big. A long scroll is the cost: 5 to 180 min in 5 min steps.

  • Repeat yesterday's 30 min walk: 1 tap, Log walk.
  • 45 min of easy yoga this morning: spin to 45, then Exercise, Yoga, When, This morning, Log. 5 taps and a spin, since yoga starts on Easy.
  • Calories update as you spin, so the number feels earned.
  • Keyboard and screen readers: the wheel is a slider, arrow keys move 5 min.
  • Effort is Easy, Moderate, Hard with the talk test, not today's per-exercise list.
Try: spin to 45, change the exercise to Yoga, then log it. Tap a number to jump to it.
