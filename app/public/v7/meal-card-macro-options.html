Meal card macros

Round 2. Once food is in a meal, its card says what that meal holds, without covering the scene. Two rules make that work: the numbers keep to the left of the header, where the scene has already faded, and the header grows by one line at most. The meal's own targets move one tap away, into a sheet.

Targets on the card, or just the data?

My recommendation: the card shows only what went in; the sheet shows it against the meal's target.

Just the data on the card Recommended

The card reports, it doesn't judge. "22 g · 55 g · 19 g · 478 kcal" is a fact anyone can read.

  • The day already has a judge: the score and the macro card above. Six meal cards each saying "of 24 g" would be six more scores.
  • Every meal's target is the coach's estimate. Showing it everywhere makes people chase each meal to the gram, which is not what the plan asks.
  • No numbers to compare means less to read and more scene visible.

Targets in the sheet

For anyone who asks "how did this meal do?", the ⓘ answers properly: each number against the meal's target, in plain words (on target, a little high, a little low), using the same ±20% the score uses.

  • The question is asked, so the answer is welcome, not pushed.
  • The sheet has the room the card doesn't: bands, splits, which food gave what.

More, behind an info icon

Every card here carries option A, the quiet line, plus a small frosted ⓘ in its top right corner, sitting on the scene without hiding it. Tap it and a sheet opens with the meal against its target. Six ways to draw that sheet, all with GoodFlip's sheet rules: title top left, circle X top right, no drag handle, never past 90% of the screen.

Or a chip, not a corner icon

The same sheet, opened from a small neutral chip under the meal's time instead of the ⓘ in the corner. The card shows no numbers; the sheet holds them all. The scene's corner stays empty, and the chip says what it opens.
