Mind pillar · Rhythm score · research, 28 Oct 2026

Is the Rhythm formula right for the user?

The idea holds: a steady night plus a calm day is good for metabolism, and the research backs it. The current maths does not yet match how a person lives a day, what a phone can measure, or what the evidence says is worth points.

What the research supports
  • Sleep regularity predicts mortality and HbA1c more strongly than sleep length (UK Biobank, 60,977 people; MESA).
  • A short morning check-in about last night is the clinical standard (Consensus Sleep Diary).
  • About 5 minutes of slow breathing a day reduces stress; the type of breathing matters little.
  • Naming your mood helps, and planned pleasant activities lift wellbeing (behavioural activation).
What breaks for the user today
  • Most users have no watch, so a phone records no sleep. Sleep must be entered by hand.
  • The score can start the morning at 70 of 100, unlike Eat and Move, which start at 0.
  • Duration counts as much as consistency, though the deck itself says "steady beats long".
  • Sleep hygiene ticks are the weakest evidence, yet carry a third of sleep.
  • When to tick wind-down, which night belongs to which day, and what happens on a missed night are all undefined.

Sleep quality · part 1

Sleep consistency

How alike your nights are: same bedtime, same wake time. It is the strongest sleep signal for metabolic health, and it needs only two times a night.

Why it matters

  • The most regular sleepers had 20 to 48% lower all-cause mortality than the least regular. Regularity predicted mortality better than duration (Windred 2024, n = 60,977).
  • Irregular sleepers had higher HbA1c (median 6.0 vs 5.7) and fasting glucose (99 vs 94 mg/dl) (MESA, Lunsford-Avery 2018).
  • Irregular sleepers had 38% higher risk of type 2 diabetes over 8 years, and sleeping enough hours did not cancel it (Chaput 2024, n = 73,630).

Coach window: bed wake

Options

A. Distance from the coach's window Recommended

Each night, how far bedtime and wake time were from the coach's times. Full marks within 30 minutes, falling to 0 at 2 hours. Average of the last 14 nights, shown from night 3.
For: one clear target to aim for, fits the coach model, works with typed times. Against: a wrong target punishes the user, so the target must be editable, as the calorie target is. The 30 and 120 minute marks are a design choice, set between published risk steps (30, 60, 90 minutes) and Apple's reported curve.

B. Your own steadiness

Spread of your sleep midpoint over 14 nights, with no target. 30 minutes or less is 100, 60 is 60, 90 or more is 0.
For: closest to the research, no target needed. Against: hard to explain, needs 7+ nights to be fair, and 2 AM to 10 AM every night scores perfectly.

C. Nights in window

Share of the last 14 nights where both times were within 45 minutes of the window.
For: easiest to explain ("10 of 14 nights on time"). Against: a 50 minute miss counts the same as 3 hours. 45 minutes is a design choice.
Sleep quality · part 2

Sleep duration

How much is enough
  • Adults 18 to 64: 7 to 9 hours. 65 and over: 7 to 8 hours (National Sleep Foundation 2015).
  • "7 or more hours on a regular basis" (AASM 2015).
  • Diabetes risk is lowest at 7 to 8 hours and rises about 9% for each hour below 7.
  • Long sleep is linked to higher risk too, but it is likely a sign of illness, not a cause. So it gets a gentle note, never a penalty.
Time in bed is not time asleep

Typed bedtime and wake time give time in bed. People also over-report: an average of 6.8 hours reported vs 6.0 measured, and short sleepers over-report most (Lauderdale 2008).

So we estimate sleep: time in bed minus time to fall asleep minus time awake in the night. These are the core items of the Consensus Sleep Diary.

When to ask

Interactive
The same three ticks, asked at night or next morning

A. Split: wind-down at night, sleep in the morning Recommended

A 10 second card at the start of wind-down, 60 to 90 minutes before bed, and the morning card for the night. Missed evening ticks can still be added next morning.
For: follows the sleep diary standard, asks while it is happening, avoids the phone at lights out. Against: two touches a day. Morning entries are done more often than evening ones (67% vs 62% in a 90 day study).

B. Morning only, like WHOOP's journal

One morning card: "Yesterday: tea after 2? Dinner late? Phone away?" plus the sleep questions.
For: one touch, no screen at night. Against: recall is weaker, and there is no nudge at the moment it matters.

C. Evening reminder, morning log

A gentle 9 PM "Time to wind down" with no logging, and the ticks in the morning card.
For: supports the habit with no screen work at night. Against: reminders alone lift logging only about 4%.
Self-care · part 1

Breathing

Slow breathing works: across 12 trials, breathwork lowered stress (effect size −0.35). In the best trial, 5 minutes a day for 28 days helped, and cyclic sighing did best. A 400 person trial found the exact pace mattered little.

Self-care · part 2

Mood check-in

  • Naming a feeling helps. Putting feelings into words calmed the brain's alarm response (Lieberman 2007). Tracking mood raised emotional self-awareness, which lowered depressive symptoms (Kauer 2012).
  • How often: real apps lose most users within weeks (median 30 day retention 3.3%). Once a day is the sustainable default. Apple separates "daily mood" from "momentary emotion".
  • Never score the answer. No app we reviewed rewards a good mood. Scoring it would punish honesty.
  • The Mind app's 7 emojis (Happy, Calm, Neutral, Sad, Stressed, Irritated, Tired) mix pleasantness with energy, so they cannot be ordered or charted.

A review found 9% of mental health apps listed a wrong crisis number, so the number must be tested regularly. The low-mood thresholds here are a design choice.
Self-care · part 3

Me-time, as a pleasant activity

The science behind me-time is behavioural activation: plan a pleasant or meaningful activity, do it, notice how it felt. It lifted wellbeing in non-clinical groups (effect size 0.52, 20 trials), and lay counsellors used it in Goa primary care.

No validated Indian list exists, so the 12 below are a design proposal. What counts: chosen for enjoyment or meaning, about 10 minutes or more, not chores, work or scrolling. Watching TV counts only if it was chosen on purpose (a design choice).

Lessons for Rhythm

  • Publish the formula. Only Apple does, and it is the easiest to trust. A typed-in score needs this even more.
  • Score actions and steadiness, never feelings. Faces and mood go to insight.
  • One morning card covers last night, pre-filled, under 15 seconds.
  • Daily length, rolling regularity. Show consistency over 14 nights, so one bad night moves it a little.
  • Say "no score yet" honestly until there are 3 nights.
  • Insight beats points. After 3 nights with and 3 without a habit, say what it did: "On nights you put the phone away, you fell asleep sooner."
  • No streak resets. The 14 night window already forgives a missed night.
Step 2

Putting it together

Option A. Steady nights, small days Recommended

Night 70 = Consistency 30 + Duration 25 + Wind-down 15
Day 30 = Mood 10 + Breathing 10 + Pleasant activity 10

Keeps the deck's 70/30 story. Regularity leads, as the evidence does. Wind-down is 3 ticks of 5. Every day action is the same simple 10.

Option B. Apple-shaped night

Night 70 = Duration 35 + Consistency 21 + Interruptions 14
Day 30 = Wind-down 10 + Mood 10 + Breathing 5 + Pleasant 5

Apple's 50/30/20 scaled to 70, with wind-down moved into the day half. Familiar to iPhone users, but length leads again.

Both: today = last night's sleep + today's day actions + tonight's wind-down. A night not logged counts 0 with a "Log last night" nudge, as an unlogged meal does on Eat. Consistency shows from night 3.

The Mind coach's framework

The coach's document builds the Mind score from four domains and 16 daily ticks. It brings in ideas the Rhythm deck lacks: stress in the moment, social connection, and a sense of achievement. But it scores only ticks, so it cannot tell a good night from a bad one.

Where it clashes with the research

  • Sleep is only ticked, never measured. "Follow planned sleep and wake time" is one tick; duration is absent. A short broken night scores full marks if the four boxes are ticked.
  • Sleep is called the foundation but weighs 30%, barely above stress and motivation at 25%. The Rhythm deck gives it 70%.
  • 16 ticks a day. The document says to keep the count low, yet 16 is more than Eat and Move together ask. Mental health apps lose most users within weeks (median 30 day retention 3.3%).
  • Relaxation is counted twice: 5 min before sleep in Sleep, 2 min in Stress.
  • 2 minutes of breathing is below the 5 minutes used in the best trial.
  • "At Risk" for a tick score means a person who skipped the app reads as clinically at risk. Bands should describe habits, not health.
  • The 1 to 10 sleep rating and the mood ratings do not say whether the value changes the score. The research says it should not.

How to combine them

A. Rhythm core, framework ideas in the right places Recommended

Keep Option A's score (night 70, day 30). The day's three slots widen: Calm = 5 min breathing or the coach's weekly CBT tool. Mood = morning check-in, with an optional trigger chip. Joy = a pleasant activity or a connection. Daily goal and achievement go to Home To-do. Stress Pause becomes an SOS button.
For: about 6 touches a day, sleep is measured, the coach's tool is in the score. Against: drops motivation from the Mind score.

B. Four small domains

Sleep 50 (consistency 25, duration 15, wind-down 10) · Calm 20 (breathing or CBT tool) · Joy 15 (mood check-in, pleasant or connection) · Drive 15 (set a goal in the morning, mark it done at night).
For: keeps the coach's four domains and names. Against: about 8 touches, and Drive overlaps with To-do.

C. The framework as written

16 ticks, 30/25/20/25.
For: clinically complete, already agreed by the coach. Against: heavy daily load, sleep not measured, labels that alarm.

Open items and unverified facts

  • Name. The app calls this pillar "Wellbeing" and the decks call it "Rhythm".
  • Shift workers and people who sleep in two blocks (afternoon nap culture). Does the coach window allow two windows?
  • Long sleep above the band is not penalised. Should the coach be alerted after a run of 10 hour nights?
  • Pleasant activity and Move: can one walk count in both pillars?
  • Chai: its caffeine content was not checked, which matters for the 2 PM rule.
  • Unverified: Apple's minutes to points curve, Oura's 2 week regularity window, Fitbit's 50/25/25 split, a minutes figure for morning daylight, a bedroom temperature figure, DDS-2 in Indian populations, the time of day breakdown of reminder effects.
  • Design choices, not research: 30 and 120 minute consistency marks, 4.5 hour duration floor, the minutes behind each chip, the 45 minute window in option C, the 12 activity list, the low mood thresholds.
  • Mind app fixes: breathing sessions never get marked complete, and the mood emojis need to become an ordered scale.
