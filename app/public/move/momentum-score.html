What the score stands on

Five things every user should understand

From the research in the Move deck and the way coaches build plans. A coach's own words go here once the fitness team signs them off.

01
The day matters, not the hour

What shapes your metabolism is what you do across the whole day, not the one hour you train. The field calls this 24-hour movement, and how activity is spread across the day is measured in its own right.

Four short moves across the daybeatsOne long session, then sitting
02
Every plan is personal

The coach sets the routines, which part of the day each one sits in, the small moves and the steps goal, from the user's goal, body and health.

03
Calories are the common unit

The coach's exercises and any workout the user logs are counted in the same unit, kcal burned. So a gym hour can stand in for a skipped routine, and nothing the user does goes uncounted.

04
The movement between counts (NEAT)

Taking the stairs, standing in a meeting, a walk after dinner. Coined at Mayo Clinic by Dr. James Levine, NEAT is one part of the picture, not the whole.

05
Doing more never counts against you

Going past a target never adds more and never takes points away. Extra effort is coached instead: move it to another part of the day and the score climbs.

System insight

Built on what the coach portal already knows

When a coach creates an exercise plan in the coach portal, every target the score needs is set in three steps.

Step 1
Exercise plan

Routines for each day, each set to a part of the day. A day needs at least 2 routines, or is marked as a rest day.

Routine 1: Morning · Routine 2: Evening
Step 2
Exercises and kcal

Each exercise has reps, sets and rest, so its duration and kcal burned are worked out.

Lunges, 6 min: 4.5 × 3.5 × 65 ÷ 200 × 6 = 31 kcal

The score

The MOVE/Momentum score

One number from 0 to 100 that answers one question:

Did you keep moving from morning to night?

Try the Momentum sandbox ↗

Four things add to it, and the score starts at 0 each morning.

40
Your workoutBurn the calories your coach planned, by doing their workout or logging your own.
40
Moving all dayEach part of the day you move in adds 10.
10
Your stepsReach your step target for the day.
10
Small movesTick off the small moves your coach set.

The maths, simply

How the score is worked out

1
Workout: 40 × kcal burned ÷ kcal planned

kcal burned is the coach's exercises done plus any workout logged by search. Each is MET × 3.5 × weight (kg) ÷ 200 × minutes. It stops at 40.

2
Steps: 10 × steps ÷ steps goal

Every step counts, from Apple Health or Health Connect, or logged by hand. It stops at 10.

3
Small moves: 10 × ticked ÷ planned

Only the small moves the coach set. Users can't add their own.

4
Moving all day: 10 × parts of the day moved in
  • CountsAn exercise done, a workout logged, or a small move ticked in that part.
  • Doesn'tSteps alone. They are one number for the whole day.
5
Add the four, then round

40 + 10 + 10 + 40 = 100. Only the total is rounded, so the parts shown on their own may be off by one.

The principles behind it

  • Moving in all four parts of the day is the only way to 100.
  • Doing the workout then sitting all day lands around 52%.
  • Any workout counts, in kcal, against the coach's plan.
  • Going past a target never adds more, and never takes away.
  • Undoing a log takes back what it added.
  • Intensity and heart rate are not measured.

Edge cases

What happens when…

How the score and the Move screen behave in the moments that are easy to get wrong.

LoggedThe user does a workout the coach didn't plan

They search and log it. Its kcal joins the coach's exercises against the day's plan, and the part of the day it was done in counts for moving all day.

LoggedThey move in a part with no routine

A logged workout or a ticked small move there still counts for moving all day. A plain card shows a + to log one.

CappedThey burn more than planned

The workout stops at 40. It never goes into minus. The win still plays, with what the log actually added.

CappedThey walk past their steps goal

Steps stop at 10. The steps card says "Goal reached. Every step from here is extra."

LoggedThey finish a routine in the player

Finish closes the player and drops the routine's exercises into the bar at the bottom, ticked, so they log together and the score steps up once.

LoggedThey log later in the day

Logging at night gives the same score as logging live, as long as the time is set to when it was done. Future times are blocked.

UndoThey untick an exercise or small move

It asks first, then takes back what it added. The score settles at its new number, with no minus and no line under it.

UndoThey remove a logged workout

"Remove this workout?" then its kcal leave the score, and the part may stop counting for moving all day.

ShownThe score changes

The number steps up with its +N%, then the line under it says "Your score went up by N%, keep logging your movement." A workout log earns FlipCoins after that.

ShownOnly workouts logged on the day keep the streak

FlipCoins come one a day for logging a workout, with +20 for a week and +50 for a month.

Still to decide

Open questions

Found while playing every case in the sandbox. Each needs a call from product and the fitness team.

01
Following the plan doesn't always score 100

On the Morning & Evening plan, a perfect day scores 90, because nothing is planned at night. On Eat, following the plan always scores 100. Should moving all day count only the parts the coach planned, or should the coach always plan something in all four parts?

02
Rest days

The coach portal can mark a day as a rest day, but the score has no rule for it yet. Does a rest day score on small moves and steps alone, or show no score?

03
A log that adds nothing

When the workout is already full and the part of the day already counts, a new log adds +0%. Do we skip the win, or say what it did instead?

04
Weight and intensity

kcal uses a fixed 65 kg and one MET per exercise for now. Real data needs the user's weight and each exercise's MET.

05
Days without a plan

Before the first consultation there is no plan to score against. Eat locks its score until a diet plan is assigned. Move should do the same.
