The Move pillar

The story of Move

We help people improve their metabolism, and every pillar needs a habit story that fits into a real day. For Move, the objective is simple: build a story that motivates people to keep moving, feels achievable in daily life, and keeps the coach at the center of it. This one-pager walks through that story, a glimpse of the experience, and the math that powers the score.

The insight

The belief we need to break.

One good workout redeems the day.

It doesn't. A workout followed by ten hours of sitting is metabolically worse than the same movement spread out. What shapes your metabolism is not the one hour you train, it is what you do throughout the day. The day is the thing that matters, not the single session inside it.

The concept

Momentum

"Moving once isn't the answer.
Moving often is."

Momentum is not a type of movement. It is a lens on the whole day. It reads everything through one question: did you keep moving from morning to night. That means it reads three things together:

  • The coach's workout, your exercise for the day
  • Your steps across the day
  • A short walk after each meal (NEAT), which is how the rest of the day gets counted

How it works

What builds your Momentum.

True of the model whichever way its arithmetic has been written. What the four are worth, and how each is earned, is the part that changed.

  • 1
    The workout, the hero
    The coach prescribes a session. It is the biggest single thing in the day and the largest single move the number makes.
  • 2
    The small movements between
    The stairs, standing through a call, a walk after a meal. No gym, no change of clothes and no half hour, placed at hours that spread the day out.
  • 3
    Steps
    Read from the phone rather than from anything you tick, so a step goal and a task list never pay for the same walk twice.
  • 4
    Where it landed
    The moat. Spread multiplies what the first three earned, so the same effort scattered across a day beats the same effort in one block.
The logic, as it first stood

The formula, in minutes.

This is where Momentum started. It is kept in place, in the order it was written, because the argument it makes is still the right one and only its arithmetic changed. What replaced it is at the end of this deck.

Momentum = Movement × Spread bonus
Movement = your moving minutes ÷ 70, the minutes your coach asked for
Spread bonus = 0.75 + 0.25 × how much of the day was moving

The 70 minutes your coach asks for

The session30 Earned by logging a session. A strength and mobility session is not walking, so steps cannot stand in for it.
Through the day40 A 10 minute walk after each meal, or the walking your phone saw in that part of the day. The larger of the two, never both.

Every minute enters this total exactly once, which is what stops the same walk being paid for twice.

The parts of the day are not worth the same

Morning15
Afternoon30
Evening35
Night20

Afternoon movement produced the largest HbA1c effect in a four year trial in people with type 2 diabetes, 30 to 50% larger than other times of day, and a walk after the evening meal beat the daily average. A part of the day counts once movement lands in it, so the bonus climbs from 0.75 to a full 1.00 as the day fills.

Going past the plan is noticed, never scored

Minutes beyond either half stay out of the daily number and pay into the other half at a fifth of face value, up to 8 minutes. So a very long walk earns a little of the session, and a long session earns a little of the day. The rest is Kaira's to talk about rather than the score's to absorb.

What changed

The argument held. The arithmetic did not.

Everything up to here is the model as it was first written, and every claim it makes is still one we would make. What went wrong was never the reasoning. It was that the model asked for things a real plan cannot always give, and marked people for decisions they never made.

Then

  • Seventy minutes, fixed, whoever the patient was.
  • Walks timed at ten minutes each, on a stopwatch nobody starts.
  • Only the prescribed session earned the session. A swim of your own scored nothing.
  • Parts of the day weighted 15 to 35, so where your coach put a task changed your score.
  • Spare minutes paid across at a fifth, capped at eight.

Now

  • ✓
    Whatever the coach assigned. Finish it and you are at 100, whatever it was.
  • ✓
    Small movements are one tap. No duration is asked for.
  • ✓
    The workout is minutes from any log, so your own movement counts as surely as the plan's.
  • ✓
    Every part of the day counts the same. Nobody picks which part their plan lands in.
  • ✓
    Overshoot is Kaira's to raise, in the hour it can still be spent in.

One thing to be honest about. The old model said a strength and mobility session is not walking, and it was right. Scored on minutes, a walk now satisfies a mobility plan, and the physio's four moves are no longer required to earn the workout. That is the price of counting movement people actually do.

The logic now

The formula, in tasks.

Momentum is how much of your plan you did, multiplied by how much of your day you did it across.

Momentum = Movement × Spread
Movement = what you finished ÷ what your coach assigned
Spread = 0.75 + 0.25 × how many parts of the assigned day you moved in
Called Spread rather than a spread bonus, because it multiplies rather than adds. It can only ever scale what was already earned, which is why one long block cannot buy its way to a high number.

Finish the plan and you are at 100. Always.

The coach assigns three kinds of thing and nothing else. Everything below is worked out from them, so no plan needs a number set by hand.

A workout× 6 Scored on minutes against the minutes the coach asked for, from any log. Deliberately the heaviest single thing in the day.
A small movement× 1 One tap. No duration is asked for, because nobody times the stairs.
A step goal× 4 Read from the phone, counted in proportion, and the only one that pays part marks.

The usual plan is a session, four small movements and a step goal. The session is deliberately the heaviest single thing in it: at parity with the small movements a finished session that happened all in one hour scored below four flights of stairs, which is true to the concept and wrong to the person who did it. Assign more or fewer of anything and the shares divide themselves again.

Every part of the day counts the same

Morning1
Afternoon1
Evening1
Night1

They were weighted for a while, 15 through 35, off the four year trial that found afternoon movement did the most for HbA1c. Good evidence in the wrong place. Nobody chooses which part of the day their tasks sit in, the coach does, so an unequal table marks two patients differently for a decision neither of them made: do your morning session and you fill 15, do the same session in the evening because that is where your physio put it and you fill 35. The science belongs in where the coach places the task, not in what the patient is scored out of. A part counts once movement lands in it, so Spread climbs from 0.75 to a full 1.00 as the day fills.

Spread is measured against the day the coach asked for, not against the clock

If a plan only reaches into the morning and the evening, then the morning and the evening are the whole of it, and finishing them is a full 1.00. This is the part that makes the score usable for somebody with back pain, or after surgery, or on a day their coach deliberately kept short. Nobody is measured against a task they were never given. A plan with no timed tasks at all, a step goal and nothing else, has nothing to spread and keeps the full 1.00.

Going past the plan is noticed, never scored

Doing more than was asked cannot take the number above 100, and it is not meant to. What it does instead is give Kaira something to say, and what she says depends on whether the day still has anywhere to put it.

A part of the day still emptyMove some of it there rather than adding more now. Spread multiplies what was already earned; another set in the same hour does not.
The plan already inStop. The day cannot pay for it any more, and tomorrow can. A score that keeps climbing past the plan is a different promise from the one the coach made.

Robustness

Why it holds up.

  • ✓
    It rewards a day, not an hour. Spread multiplies rather than adds, so it can only scale what was earned. It cannot invent marks, and it cannot be bought by moving more in one block.
  • ✓
    One workout is not a whole day. "Do the session then sit all day" lands around 34%. The session is deliberately the heaviest single thing a coach assigns, so it clears the four small movements on its own, and spread still keeps it to a third of the day: adding one walk after dinner takes the same session to 39%. What a session cannot do is reach the whole day without the rest of the plan.
  • ✓
    Over-achievers get coached, not ignored. Spare minutes pay a little into the other half of the plan, and Kaira reads the rest against the clock, so the advice is only ever about a part of the day still to come.
  • ✓
    The coach stays the hero. The coach sets the 70 minutes, so the coach defines what 100 means. The target is personal because a person wrote it.
