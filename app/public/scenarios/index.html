To-do, EM3 and the streak

Only the moments that live in these three things, written as questions rather than answers. The middle column is the question the design has to answer. The right column is what happens today, and it is blank wherever we have not decided yet. Ideas for the blanks sit in Suggestions.

STAGE 09

The streak

Meant to build steadiness. The risk is that it quietly becomes the thing people are afraid of losing.

The momentThe question we have to answerWhat happens today
I see a flame with a number next to it. What is it counting: days, tasks, or pillars? Can I tell without being told? Days where the whole list was finished. The fill shows today's progress.
I am on day one. How do we make one day feel like a beginning rather than a small number? Day 1 of your streak, sitting above the four rings.
I have both a streak and four rings on the same screen. Are these two facts or one fact said twice? One block. The streak line sits on top of the rings.
I nearly finish, then run out of day. Does almost count for anything, or is it all or nothing? Nothing. The day simply does not count.
I miss a day. What do we say, in whose voice, and how quickly do we offer a way back? The streak reads as ended.
I am ill for three days. Is there any protection, and does knowing about it change how people behave? Blank
I reach seven days. What is a week worth, and is the reward the point or the recognition? A milestone becomes claimable, with coins.
I have kept it for sixty days. Does the streak keep mattering, or should it hand over to something else? Blank
My coach adds four more tasks to my day. Is a harder day worth the same one as an easier day? Yes. A day is a day.
FOR STAGE 01

My first time on To-do

The blanks: what this list is, how long the wait is, what the first tick teaches, and whether day two of waiting looks different.

Name the list in the person's own terms Small

One line under the header, once: these are the habits your coaches will build your plan around. It answers whose list this is without another explainer screen.

Put a real promise on the waiting card Small

Instead of on the way, say who and when: your diet plan lands after your coach reads your first three days. A wait with a condition attached feels like progress; an open-ended one feels like being forgotten.

Make the waiting card count down Medium

Two of three days logged, so the card becomes a progress bar towards the plan. The starter days stop being filler and become the input.

Teach on the first tick, not before Small

The first completed task of a person's life in the app gets one extra line: that is one habit in, your coach can see it. After that, nothing.

If we only do one: the waiting card that counts down. It answers the wait, the why and the what next in a single card people already look at.
FOR STAGE 02

My plan arrives

The blanks: how I find out, a half handover, meeting options for the first time, spotting new rows, and later changes.

Treat arrival as an event, not a state change Medium

A push, then a one-time card at the top of To-do: your plan is in. Tapping it opens what your coach set, meal by meal and session by session. It closes for good once read.

Let the coach say it, not the system Medium

The arrival note comes from the named coach in chat, and the To-do card quotes one line of it. The plan feels handed over by a person, which is the whole point of the program.

Mark new rows for one day Small

A quiet new label on rows that were not there yesterday, gone by the next morning. It also covers plan changes later, using the same rule.

Introduce options on first contact Small

The first meal row that carries choices explains itself in one line: your coach gave you two ways to eat this, pick the one you had. Once only.

Say which half arrived Small

The waiting card already has a diet tag and an exercise tag. Let one go green and one stay waiting, so a half handover reads as progress rather than a bug.

If we only do one: the coach's own arrival message with a card that opens the plan. Everything else here is polish on top of it.
FOR STAGE 03

Seeing EM3 inside my day

The blanks: whether colour alone teaches the mapping, and whether one full pillar is worth anything.

Colour carries it, words teach it once Small

Keep the coloured circle as the everyday signal, but on the first day only, each pillar's first row says its name beside the time. Four small labels, one day, and the colour is learned for good.

Make the rings a filter, not just a summary Medium

Tap Eat at the bottom and the day dims to its Eat rows. It teaches the grouping by showing it, keeps the list chronological, and gives the rings a second job.

Name the pillar on the way in Small

When a row sends me to Eat, the screen I land on says this is where your food lives. The mapping is taught at the moment it is being used.

Let a pillar close for the day Medium

When every Eat task is done, the ring fills and says Eat is done for today. Four small wins across a day beat one at the end, and it makes the frame visible without a lecture.

Say the frame in the weekly read, not the daily one Bigger

The day stays chronological. The week is where EM3 does the talking: your Move was strong, your Mind was thin. The frame becomes an insight rather than a filing system.

If we only do one: let a pillar close for the day. It rewards partial days, which is most days, and it teaches the frame by using it.
FOR STAGE 04

Doing a task from To-do

The blanks: knowing where a tap leads, two finishes at once, and one action closing two rows.

Two shapes, two meanings Small

Rows that tick in place look like checkboxes. Rows that take me somewhere carry a quiet arrow at the end. One glance tells me whether tapping costs me a screen.

Scale the celebration to the effort Medium

Three tiers instead of one: a tick fills and strikes, a logged meal or session gets the confetti, the last task of the day gets the full screen. Right now a capsule and a workout get the same fanfare, which flattens both.

Collect finishes that arrive together Medium

Two meals logged in one visit produce one moment: two things done, six of nine today. Two identical toasts in a row read as a bug.

Show one action closing two rows Medium

When a walk closes both the session and the step target, say so once: that walk covered two things today. It makes the day feel generous rather than accidental.

If we only do one: scale the celebration. It is the difference between delight and noise, and it costs no new screens.
FOR STAGE 05

Doing the thing without going through To-do

The blanks: whether the toast teaches the link, silent syncs, catching up on what happened while I was away, and finishing a day off-screen.

The toast should name the frame once Small

The first few times, say done for today, one of your Eat habits. After a week, drop back to the short version. It teaches the connection at the exact moment it happens.

Make the toast a door Small

Tapping it opens To-do at that row. People who never visit the list get a reason to, at the moment they are feeling good about it.

A quiet welcome back line Medium

Opening To-do after things finished elsewhere: two things ticked while you were away. It closes the loop for anyone who lives in Eat and Move.

Let the day's moment follow me Medium

If the last task finishes inside Eat, the full screen moment plays there. The reward should happen where the person is, not wait on a screen they may not open.

Stay silent for things I did not do in the app Small

A step target closed by a background sync gets no toast. Celebrating something the phone noticed, not something I chose, cheapens the ones I earned.

If we only do one: let the day's completion moment follow the person. It is the single biggest payoff in the product and it currently depends on being on the right screen.
FOR STAGE 06

Knowing where I stand mid-day

The blanks: a moment that has passed, nudging without pressure, and noticing a good day early.

Let the hour pass quietly Small

A row whose time has gone loses its time stamp rather than turning red. Still open, never late. Nothing in a health app should scold someone for eating at three instead of one.

One nudge a day, from Kaira, with a reason Medium

Not a reminder per task. One line in the evening that knows something: your dinner is the last one today, and it is the meal your coach is watching.

Notice the good day before it ends Medium

At two thirds done, one line: this is your best day this week. Recognition on the way up is worth more than a summary after the fact.

Make the count the headline, not the list Small

For a mid-day glance, four of nine and what is next are the only two facts that matter. Everything else can stay below the fold.

If we only do one: notice the good day early. It is the clearest way to build steadiness without the streak doing the pushing.
FOR STAGE 07

Saying not today

The blanks: a repeated skip, a day of nothing but skips, and travel.

A repeated skip becomes a question, not a warning Medium

The fourth time in a week: you have passed on this most days. Want your coach to swap it for something else? A skip that keeps happening is feedback about the plan, not about the person.

Give a lighter day a name Bigger

One tap at the top: make today lighter. It keeps the essentials, drops the rest, and the day can still be completed. Better than fifteen individual skips, and honest about the fact that some days are like that.

Decide the all-skipped rule and say it out loud Small

The proposal: a day needs at least one thing actually done to count. Written into the streak guide in one plain line, so nobody has to guess.

Let a skip carry a reason, optionally Medium

Two or three taps in the sheet: travelling, not well, not today. It gives the coach something real to work with and costs the person almost nothing.

If we only do one: the lighter day. It is the honest answer to travel, illness and bad weeks, and it protects the streak's meaning instead of eroding it.
FOR STAGE 08

Finishing the day

The blanks: doing more after finishing, and whether a finished day is worth sharing.

Let the day stay open after it is done Small

Extra water, an extra walk: recorded, counted in the pillar, but no second celebration. Finishing should never mean stopping.

Vary the closing line Small

The full screen moment says something slightly different on day one, day seven and day thirty. The same sentence every night stops being read by the third one.

Save sharing for milestones Small

A finished day is between me and my coach. A week or a month is worth showing someone. Keeping the difference protects both.

End the day with one thing to carry Medium

One line from Kaira in the closing moment that is about today specifically, not a summary. It is the one place an insight is guaranteed to be read.

If we only do one: vary the closing line. The moment is already built, and repetition is the only thing that will kill it.
FOR STAGE 09

The streak

The blanks: protection when life happens, and what the streak becomes after it has done its job.

Say the rule where the number is Small

Under the flame, once: finish everything on your day to keep it. Nobody should have to open a guide to learn what a number on their home screen means.

Earn protection rather than being given it Medium

Every seven days banks one rest day, up to two held at a time. A missed day spends one automatically and tells you it did. Protection you earned is a reward; protection handed out for free removes the meaning.

Count almost, quietly Medium

A day at eight of nine does not keep the streak, but it is not nothing either. Remember it, and let a week of near misses show up as what it is: a consistent person having a busy week.

Break it gently and offer the way back in the same breath Small

Not your streak ended. Rather: you were on eleven days. Today starts the next one. The number that mattered gets remembered, and the next action is right there.

Let the streak hand over after a month Bigger

Past thirty days the daily count stops being the story and the pillars take over: your Move has held for six weeks. The streak's job is to build the habit, and then to get out of the way.

Never let a bigger plan mean a riskier streak Small

If a coach adds four tasks, the day got harder but the reward did not change. Worth deciding explicitly, because it is the quickest way for a streak to start punishing the people doing the most.

If we only do one: earned rest days. It is the single change that keeps a streak motivating instead of frightening, and it fits the sustainable habits idea rather than fighting it.
