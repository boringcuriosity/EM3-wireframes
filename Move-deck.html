The Move pillar

The story of Move

We help people improve their metabolism, and every pillar needs a habit story that fits into a real day. For Move, the objective is simple: build a story that motivates people to keep moving, feels achievable in daily life, and keeps the coach at the center of it. This one-pager walks through that story, a glimpse of the experience, and the math that powers the score.

The insight

The belief we need to break.

One good workout redeems the day.

It doesn't. A workout followed by ten hours of sitting is metabolically worse than the same movement spread out. What shapes your metabolism is not the one hour you train, it is what you do throughout the day. The day is the thing that matters, not the single session inside it.

The concept

Momentum

"Moving once isn't the answer.
Moving often is."

Momentum is not a type of movement. It is a lens on the whole day. It reads everything through one question: did you keep moving from morning to night. That means it reads three things together:

  • The coach's workout, your exercise for the day
  • Your steps across the day
  • The small movements in between (NEAT)

How it works

What builds your Momentum.

  • 1
    Exercise, the hero
    The coach's prescribed session, or a logged substitute. Weighted highest, because it bridges coach and user.
  • 2
    Steps
    Your everyday locomotion against a target, captured automatically from Health.
  • 3
    NEAT
    Small coach-assigned actions across the day: take the stairs, walk after lunch, a stretch at night.
  • 4
    Spread across the day
    The moat. Movement in Morning, Afternoon, Evening and Night beats the same effort crammed into one block.
The logic

The math, in one line.

Momentum = Exercise (40) + Steps (10) + NEAT (10) + Spread (40)

The Spread (40) is earned across each part of the day

Stay active in a part of the day and it adds 10%. Move across all four and the full 40% is yours. That is how staying active throughout the day, not just once, earns the score.

Robustness

Why it holds up.

  • ✓
    It rewards a day, not an hour. A workout crammed into one block scores lower than the same effort spread out. The concept is built into the math.
  • ✓
    It cannot be faked with one workout. "Do the session then sit all day" lands around 52%, a visibly incomplete day, not a pass.
  • ✓
    Over-achievers get coached, not ignored. Extra effort past target is celebrated and redirected: "put it in your afternoon and your score climbs."
  • ✓
    The coach stays the hero. Exercise carries the most weight, so the plan the coach sets is always the biggest lever the user can pull.
