The Move pillar

The story of Move

We help people improve their metabolism, and every pillar needs a habit story that fits into a real day. For Move, the objective is simple: build a story that motivates people to keep moving, feels achievable in daily life, and keeps the coach at the center of it. This one-pager walks through that story, a glimpse of the experience, and the math that powers the score.

The insight

The belief we need to break.

One good workout redeems the day.

It doesn't. A workout followed by ten hours of sitting is metabolically worse than the same movement spread out. What shapes your metabolism is not the one hour you train, it is what you do throughout the day. The day is the thing that matters, not the single session inside it.

The concept

Momentum

"Moving once isn't the answer.
Moving often is."

Momentum is not a type of movement. It is a lens on the whole day. It reads everything through one question: did you keep moving from morning to night. That means it reads three things together:

  • The coach's workout, your exercise for the day
  • Your steps across the day
  • A short walk after each meal (NEAT), which is how the rest of the day gets counted

How it works

What builds your Momentum.

  • 1
    The session, the hero
    The coach prescribes one exercise session a day. It is 30 of the 70 minutes, and only a logged session earns it.
  • 2
    A walk after each meal
    Ten minutes, four times. This is the other 40 minutes, and it is where a day is either kept moving or lost.
  • 3
    Steps
    Read per part of the day. Where your phone saw more walking than the walk you ticked, the larger of the two counts, never both.
  • 4
    Where it landed
    The moat. Spread multiplies what the first three earned, and afternoon and evening count for more than morning does.
The logic

The formula.

Momentum = Movement × Spread bonus
Movement = your moving minutes ÷ 70, the minutes your coach asked for
Spread bonus = 0.75 + 0.25 × how much of the day was moving

The 70 minutes your coach asks for

The session30 Earned by logging a session. A strength and mobility session is not walking, so steps cannot stand in for it.
Through the day40 A 10 minute walk after each meal, or the walking your phone saw in that part of the day. The larger of the two, never both.

Every minute enters this total exactly once, which is what stops the same walk being paid for twice.

The parts of the day are not worth the same

Morning15
Afternoon30
Evening35
Night20

Afternoon movement produced the largest HbA1c effect in a four year trial in people with type 2 diabetes, 30 to 50% larger than other times of day, and a walk after the evening meal beat the daily average. A part of the day counts once movement lands in it, so the bonus climbs from 0.75 to a full 1.00 as the day fills.

Going past the plan is noticed, never scored

Minutes beyond either half stay out of the daily number and pay into the other half at a fifth of face value, up to 8 minutes. So a very long walk earns a little of the session, and a long session earns a little of the day. The rest is Kaira's to talk about rather than the score's to absorb.

Robustness

Why it holds up.

  • ✓
    It rewards a day, not an hour. Spread multiplies rather than adds, so it can only scale what was earned. It cannot invent marks, and it cannot be bought by moving more in one block.
  • ✓
    It cannot be faked with one workout. "Do the session then sit all day" lands around 36%, because the plan is a day of movement and the session is 30 minutes of it.
  • ✓
    Over-achievers get coached, not ignored. Spare minutes pay a little into the other half of the plan, and Kaira reads the rest against the clock, so the advice is only ever about a part of the day still to come.
  • ✓
    The coach stays the hero. The coach sets the 70 minutes, so the coach defines what 100 means. The target is personal because a person wrote it.
